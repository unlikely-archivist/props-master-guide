# Fake Food Master Guide

A working synthesis of **_The Fake Food Cookbook: Props You Can't Eat for Theatre, Film, and TV_** by Karestin Harrison and Tamara L. Honesty (Routledge/Focal Press, 2018; ISBN 978-1-138-21227-5).

The book is laid out like a cookbook, one dish per chapter. This guide puts the same material into a form a props shop can use. Parts 1–6 cover each material and technique once, with the numbers that matter. Part 7 has condensed recipe cards for all 41 dishes. Parts 8–10 are quick references: a shopping list, troubleshooting, and which dishes suit which shows.

> **Currency warning.** Prices, product names, and formulas date from 2018. Some had already changed by then: Montana GOLD "Smash Potato" was discontinued, and 3M Super 77's formula changed. None of this has been re-checked since. Check prices, availability, and current Safety Data Sheets before buying. The chemistry and techniques themselves are stable.

## 1. Real vs. fake: when to build

**Build fake food when:**
- **The run is long.** For weeks at a regional theatre or months on Broadway, making a dish once is cheaper than buying perishables every night.
- **The food is set dressing, or actors mime eating it.** No crew time is spent cooking.
- **It sits out for hours** on a film or photo set. Real food spoils, attracts insects, and breaks continuity from take to take and day to day.
- **You need a look you can't buy.** Commercial fake food is expensive and rarely the right shape. Changing colour or shape during your own build is easier than altering a mass-produced item.
- **It can come back next year.** The roast goose for an annual *A Christmas Carol* can be pulled from storage and only needs freshening.

**Use real food (or a real/fake hybrid) when:**
- The run is very short.
- An actor actually has to eat it (see the [removable edible cake piece](#39-cake-with-a-removable-edible-piece)).
- Build time is extremely short. Drinks are the most common case.

**A hybrid example.** A touring *Diary of Anne Frank* (Stage One) needed green beans snapped on stage every night. The crew cast the beans in green wax. Snapped wax beans sound right, and the broken pieces get remelted into the next batch. The tour carried the molds and a toaster oven and recast whenever it stayed two or more days in one venue.

**Storage (applies to everything).** Make sure the piece is completely dry. Put it in a zip bag, press out all the air, and seal it. Then put the bag in a plastic bin with a tight lid. Stored this way, pieces last for years. Baked salt dough lasts at least a year. The authors' gel-wax hot chocolate and Encapso K lemonade still looked new after five years.

## 2. Core principles

1. **Research first, every time.** Buy the real item or pull reference photos. Realism comes from matching **colour, shape, texture, and sheen**, and most fakes fail on sheen.
2. **Realistic paint is built from layers of translucent glazes.** Base coat, then several transparent layers: amber shellac, FEV, Glossy Wood Tone, tinted gel mediums. A single opaque coat looks like plastic.
3. **Prime or seal foam before painting it.** Paint put straight onto foam dries brittle and flakes off when handled. You can skip priming only if the prop is never touched.
4. **Shop tools never go back to the kitchen.** Pans, spoons, measuring cups, and graters used for fake food get labelled (for example, "WAX ONLY") and are never used for real food again.
5. **Think about distance from the audience.** Scale and detail depend on how close the audience is. A stylised show may need a different scale, and the book suggests this for *If You Take a Mouse to the Movies*.
6. **Cast instead of carving when you need many.** Make one mold from a real item (wing, shrimp, rib bone, corn cob, bread roll, pineapple ring) and you can produce as many copies as the show needs.
7. **Bought fakes can be improved.** Plastic lemon slices and acrylic ice cubes save time. They usually look fake because the finish is wrong (too glossy, too dull, too bright, too smooth). Re-texture and re-tone them.
8. **Repurpose.** A foam ham from *The Best Christmas Pageant Ever* was sliced up to become roast beef. Breakfast food built for *Spitfire Grill* can dress *Bus Stop*, *Diner*, or *Waitress*.
9. **Sample kits go a long way.** One Smooth-On trial kit (under $35 in 2018) often covered several dishes. One Rosco Off Broadway or Supersaturated 1-oz sample kit ($70–$100 in 2018) covers almost every recipe in the book.
10. **There's no single right way.** The recipes are starting points. Swap equivalent products freely.

## 3. Safety

### Personal protective equipment checklist
- **Eye protection.** Safety glasses or chemical goggles. Add a face shield when carving or shaping foam with power tools.
- **Hearing protection** for the jig saw, orbital sander, Dremel, and band saw.
- **Gloves**, chosen from a chemical-resistance glove chart (links in [Resources](#11-resources)). Nitrile is the general default. **Use vinyl, not latex, with Dragon Skin and Encapso K**, because latex stops them curing.
- **Long sleeves, long pants, closed-toe shoes, and an apron.** No loose clothing, scarves, or dangling jewellery.
- **First-aid kit and eyewash station** nearby.
- **Ventilation.** Use an exhaust fan or spray hood for aerosols, shellac, solvent lacquers, liquid latex that contains ammonia, and all Smooth-On products.

### Standing rules
- **Read the Safety Data Sheet (SDS) for every product, and re-read it periodically.** Manufacturers change formulas, so a product that was safe once isn't guaranteed to stay safe. (The publisher's companion site hosts SDS links for the book's products.)
- **Ask the whole cast and crew about food allergies and sensitivities**, especially when any real food is involved.
- **Shellac and denatured alcohol are flammable.** Store them in a flammables cabinet.
- **Dispose of used blades safely**, either in a sharps container or wrapped in tape.
- **Hot glue is a temporary adhesive** and can cause serious burns. Always put something under the gun's tip.

### Wax rules (apply to every wax recipe)
- Never pour wax down a drain.
- Never leave melting wax unattended. Keep it away from open flame.
- Monitor it with a candle thermometer. Gel and creme wax melt at about 130°F. Pour at 160–170°F. **Never exceed 220°F.**
- Use a potholder. Glassware filled with hot wax gets very hot.
- Keep an **ABC fire extinguisher** within reach.

### Smooth-On rules
- Wear gloves and safety glasses, and work with good ventilation. Don't inhale vapours while mixing.
- Castings get hot as they cure. Smooth-Cast 300 gets warm. Smooth-Cast 320 gets *extremely* hot and gives off visible fumes.
- Encapso K must never go on skin.

## 4. Materials playbook

### 4.1 Salt dough

Cheap modelling dough made from pantry ingredients, hardened by baking or microwaving. It's best for **crusts, fried and dumpling-type foods, and mashed potato**.

| Version | Flour | Salt | Water | Veg. oil | Alum | Method | Character / used for |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Traditional | 2 c | 1 c | ¾ c | — | — | Mix in a bowl, knead, bake at 350°F | Basic. More prone to cracking |
| V1 (cold mix) | 2 c | 2 c | 1 c | 2 tbsp | 2 tbsp | Stir together, knead | Oil and alum resist cracking |
| V2 (stovetop) | 2 c | 2 c | 1 c | 2 tbsp | 2 tbsp | Heat on low until it looks like mashed potato, then knead | Less sticky to roll. Extra salt plus oil gives a **pockmarked "fried" surface** (egg rolls, cherry pie, coconut cream pie) |
| V3 (low salt) | 2 c | 1 c | 1 c | 2 tbsp | 2 tbsp | Stovetop, as V2 | Stays soft and flexible longer while kneading (apple tart, pierogi, quiche) |
| Wonton | 2 c | much less | — | 2 tbsp | 4 tbsp alum *or* cream of tartar | Stovetop, then microwave | Flexible. Gets a boiled look |

**Key points**
- **Oil and alum** stop cracking while you roll. Alum also preserves the dough. Cream of tartar can stand in for alum. You can leave the alum out if you'll seal the dough right away (the mashed potatoes).
- **Stovetop method.** Mix the dry ingredients in a saucepan, add the water and oil, and stir over low heat for about 8 minutes until it reaches mashed-potato consistency with no lumps. Turn it out onto a floured, non-porous surface and knead it smooth.
- **Baking temperature controls puffing:**
  - 350°F for 20–25 minutes: puffs and bubbles. Fine for lattice strips and rustic crusts.
  - 250°F for 15 minutes: little puffing (cherry pie).
  - 200°F for 60 minutes: no bubbling (apple tart). Any salt dough recipe can use a low temperature for longer.
  - Take the crust out when it turns **white and dry**.
- **Firming the underside of a crust.** Shellac the cooled crust, flip it out, set it upside-down over the inverted pan on a cookie sheet, and bake it another 5–15 minutes (or let it air-dry). Then shellac the underside too.
- **Microwave method (boiled look).** Wrap the piece in a damp paper towel and microwave for 15 seconds. Pour water over it and microwave another 30 seconds. The result has the slick look of a boiled wonton. To simply dry a piece quickly, microwave it for 15 seconds.
- **Fried texture trick.** Brush amber shellac on fresh salt dough, then seal it in a zip bag for 24 hours. The shellac reacts with the moisture and salt and turns granular, like a fried egg roll.
- **Storage.** Unbaked dough keeps about 3 days airtight, refrigerated or not. Baked dough keeps at least a year if bagged and binned.

### 4.2 Candle wax (gel, creme, soy)

Used for **sauces, syrups, cheeses, custards, fillings, cold drinks, gelatin, and egg yolk**. Yaley **Gel Wax** is translucent, has a realistic weight, and is durable, odourless, and long-lasting. Yaley **Creme Wax** is opaque and sets faster.

| Blend | Result | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| Gel only | Translucent, glossy, pourable | Soy sauce, syrup, gravy, beer, coffee, hot chocolate, caramel, cocktail sauce, egg yolk, egg-white glaze, cherry filling, custard, gelatin |
| 2 gel : 1 creme | Softer, can be cut with an ordinary knife | Swiss cheese, diced tomatoes, softer quiche filling |
| 1 gel : 1 creme | Firmer, more structure, fewer bubbles | Cheddar, gouda, tomato slices, gelatin mold |
| 2 gel : 1 creme by block size (4" gel block + 2" creme block) | Firm, cut with a hot knife | Brie |
| Creme only | Opaque and solid. Grates well | Shredded cheddar, corn on the cob (cast) |
| Soy candle beads | Melts low (about 110°F; pour at 115–120°F; max 150°F). Already white. Vanilla-scented beads add a light scent | Sticky-bun icing drizzle |

**Rule of thumb:** more creme sets faster but is harder to cut once hard.

**Working with wax**
- **Temperature.** It melts at about 130°F. Pour at 160–170°F (150–170°F is fine for small pours). Maximum 220°F.
  - **To get bubbles** (beer), heat to about **215°F**. Hotter wax traps more bubbles as it cools.
  - Adding creme to gel *reduces* bubbles.
- **Colour.** Shave in Yaley concentrated candle dye. All Yaley dyes work with gel wax. On a tight budget, shaved crayon works well.
  - Test colour by pouring a little onto a ceramic dish, then adjust.
  - Dye goes a long way. For soy sauce, half a block of brown is enough.
- **Molds.** Use ceramic baking dishes, ramekins, cake pans, muffin tins, plastic Easter eggs, or gelatin molds. Spray them with Universal Mold Release first.
  - Don't move a mold until the wax has set (about 30 minutes or more for corn, 2 hours for cheese wheels, 3–4 hours for a gelatin mold).
  - A freezer speeds setting for small pieces (egg yolk).
- **Cutting.**
  - **Hot knife**, working on wax paper: it gives smooth faces. The melt drips and puddles, so lift the pieces before the puddle sets.
  - A **snap-off blade run under hot water** works for clean slices (hard-boiled egg).
  - Soft 2:1 wax cuts with an ordinary knife.
  - **Drill bits** make Swiss cheese holes; the wax won't crumble. Use several sizes.
- **Inclusions.** Wax works as a suspension medium. Use it for marshmallows, plastic grapes as cherries, wood shavings as coconut or horseradish, seed beads as tomato seeds, and paper "spinach" and "onion" in quiche. Push some pieces under and leave some floating.
- **As an adhesive.** Melted gel wax attaches pineapple rings and cherries to the cake.
- **Sealing.** Seal set gel wax with clear shellac (or Premiere Clear) before putting foam on it. Otherwise **the dye bleeds into the foam**. This applies to the beer head, Irish coffee cream, and egg yolk on foam egg white.
- **Unmolding gelatin.** Dip the mold in *warm* (not hot) water for about 10 seconds. Moisten the plate first so you can slide the gelatin into position.
- **Cleanup.** Let gel wax cool completely and it peels out of the pan.

### 4.3 Rigid foam (XPS) and laminating

Extruded polystyrene insulation board, either pink (Owens Corning Foamular) or blue (Dow Styrofoam). It's the base for **bread, cakes, roasts, ham, turkey, salami, potatoes, and filler blocks**.
- Use **1" sheet** for carved food; it gives better results. The ½" contractor packs are cost-effective for potatoes and tart bases.
- Peel the plastic film off both faces before gluing.
- **Pink foam reduces the need for a base coat** on meats. A thin earth-umber wash over pink reads as roast beef.

**Laminating adhesives**

| Adhesive | Notes |
|---|---|
| Titebond II Premium Wood Glue | Strong initial tack and cures faster than white glue. Wait at least 1 hour before shaping. Full cure takes 8 hours (24–36 hours for big turkey stacks) |
| Elmer's Glue-All | Cheap and easy to find. Use plenty and clamp well |
| Rosco FlexBond | Flexible bond. Also good for attaching parts |
| 3M Fastbond 30NF (water-based contact adhesive, "green glue") | Coat **both** faces and let them dry until no longer glossy (about 30 minutes). Assemble within 4 hours; the longer you wait in that window, the stronger the bond. Hard to wash out, so wrap the brush or roller in plastic between uses and throw it away when done. Wear gloves. **Preferred:** it cleans up easily and doesn't eat foam |
| 3M Super 77 **Classic** or **78 Polystyrene** spray | Spray both faces and let them tack for at least 30 seconds before joining. **The new Super 77 formula contains acetone and dissolves XPS. Don't use it** |

- **Clamping.** Put lauan, Masonite, or plywood cauls between the clamps and the foam so it doesn't dent. For big stacks, use a ratchet strap with wood blocks under the strap.
- **Aerosol tip.** After every use, turn the can upside down and spray until it runs clear so the nozzle doesn't clog.

**Shaping.** Rough-cut with a band saw, jig saw, or Japanese pull saw. Shape with a Surform shaver or pocket plane, or a rasp. A Dremel handles fine detail and cavities. An orbital or palm sander, or sandpaper, smooths the surface.
- **Rasped surfaces look like meat** and hide the glue lines.
- Cartoon (sketch) the shape from your reference before cutting.

**Priming before paint.** Use FoamCoat (can be sanded once dry), FlexCoat, CrystalGel, Sculpt or Coat, FlexBond, Fastbond, or Elmer's. See [4.8](#48-coatings-primers-and-adhesive-gels).

### 4.4 Soft foams: upholstery foam and backer rod
- **Flexible polyurethane foam** ("upholstery" or "squishy" foam, from fabric stores) works for **sandwich bread**. Split 1" foam to ½" for club sandwich slices, dip it in thinned off-white paint, and paint the crust edges.
- **Backer rod** (low-density polyethylene, the same family as Ethafoam) about 1⅜" in diameter makes **kielbasa-style sausage**. Prime it with FlexCoat before painting. For shorter links, stuff upholstery foam or batting into pantyhose.

### 4.5 Smooth-On mold-making and casting

Smooth-On products appear throughout because most mix **1:1 by volume with no scale**, are thin enough to pour easily, and come in trial kits. The book assumes you know basic mold-making. Smooth-On's own tutorials (smooth-on.com/tutorials) and Eric Hart's *The Prop Building Guidebook* fill that gap.

**Mold rubbers**

| Product | Mix | Working time | Demold | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| OOMOO 25 | 1A:1B by volume (pink + blue = purple) | 15 min | 75 min | Shrimp, pineapple rings |
| OOMOO 30 | 1A:1B | 30 min | 6 hr | Chicken wings, corn on the cob, rib bones |
| Dragon Skin 10 Fast | 1A:1B, stir 3 min | ~8 min | 75 min (faster above 73°F) | Bread roll (sticky buns). Very strong and stretchy |

**Casting resins**

| Product | Mix | Pot life | Demold | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Smooth-Cast 300 | 1A:1B | 3 min | 15 min | Clear liquid that cures bright white. Captures fine detail and is almost bubble-free. Gets warm. One trial kit made about 12–15 wings or 24+ rib bones |
| Smooth-Cast 320 | 1A:1B | ~3 min | 10–15 min | Ultra-low viscosity, off-white, resists moisture. **Very hot, visible fumes.** Full cure 4–6 hours |

**Standard one-piece block mold workflow**
1. **Prep the original.** Wash off any sauce. Seal porous or organic originals (real food) with **Krylon Crystal Clear Acrylic** or shellac. Unsealed food makes the mold fail.
2. **Build a mold box** from foam-core, hot-glued, **1–2" larger than the object on all sides**. Seal the seams with a thick bead of hot glue and tape the outside joints.
3. **Hot-glue the original firmly** to the floor of the box.
4. **Release.** Spray the object and box liberally with **Ease Release 200** and let it dry 5–10 minutes.
5. **Mix** in a container bigger than the volume you need so there's room to stir. Stir until the colour is uniform, scraping the sides and bottom.
6. **Pour** in one spot at the lowest point and let the rubber find its own level; a steady flow traps less air. Cover the highest point by at least ½–1" so the mold doesn't tear when you demold it.
7. **Demold.** Cut the box apart at the glued corners. Make **relief cuts** if the shape is curved or undercut (wings, shrimp, corn).
   - For wax casting, keep relief cuts to a minimum because wax leaks out of them.
8. **Tape the mold closed** with **medical tape**. Masking tape and Scotch tape failed.
9. **Release the mold again** before casting (Ease Release 200, twice with 5 minutes between coats, for Smooth-Cast).
10. **Cast.** Smooth-Cast can be tinted a little at a time with Silc Pig dropped from an eyedropper. Creme wax can be poured straight into OOMOO.
11. **Clean** food residue out of the mold with a disinfecting wipe.

**Release agent matrix**

| Situation | Use |
|---|---|
| Silicone molds (OOMOO, Dragon Skin) and general casting | Ease Release 200 |
| Urethane foam (FlexFoam-iT!) | Ease Release 2831, or Universal *followed by* 2831 |
| Wax into ceramic or metal | Universal Mold Release |
| Encapso K poured into glass you want to reuse | Petroleum jelly or Ease Release 200 |
| Teflon-coated bakeware with foam | None needed |
| **Never** | Universal or any silicone-based release *alone* on urethane foam. It collapses or shrivels the foam |

**Sources for originals.** Buy them at the grocery store (frozen shrimp, corn, dried pineapple), save them from a restaurant meal (rib bones; a lobster shell, frozen until needed), or use a commercial plastic model (bread roll, lobster).
- **Real cooked lobster is too fragile to mold.** A plastic lobster (about $6 online) worked best.

### 4.6 Expanding urethane foam (FlexFoam-iT!)

Used for **bread, cake, doughnuts, muffins, pancakes, egg white, beer head, whipped cream, and froth**.

| | FlexFoam-iT! III | FlexFoam-iT! V |
|---|---|---|
| Mix ratio (by volume) | **1A : 2B** | **1A : 1B** |
| Expansion | ~15× (lowest density) | ~11× |
| Pot life | ~35 s | ~50 s (the muffin chapter quotes 90 s; plan on 50) |
| Tacky / demold / full cure | Tacky at 30 min; set at 2 hr | Tacky at 15 min; muffins demolded at 40 min; set at 2 hr |
| Surface | More pockmarked (good for chocolate cake doughnut and rye crumb) | Smoother (plain cake doughnut, muffins) |
| Used for | Rye loaf, chocolate doughnuts, fried-egg white, pancakes, Irish coffee cream, pineapple upside-down cake | Plain doughnuts, oatmeal muffins, sticky buns, beer head, piña colada froth |

**Procedure**
1. Shake or stir each part well in its own container. **Pre-mix Part B with a drill mixer** for best results.
2. Measure with identical clear cups and use the cup ridges as a gauge. Small 3-oz cups are good for single pieces. For III, three identical cups make the 1:2 ratio easy: one cup of A, two of B.
3. Add pigment (Silc Pig brown for chocolate and rye) after combining. Mix fast and hard for **15–30 seconds**, scraping the sides and bottom.
4. **Pour immediately**, with no pause and no splashing.
5. **Fill cavities only partway.** The foam expands 11–15×, so leave headroom in a glass too.

**Surface tricks**
- **Lay wax paper over the pour** to stop a smooth skin forming and get a pock-marked crust (rye loaf). Leaving it uncovered so the top collapses gives a nicely irregular bread top.
- Spoon it instead of pouring for irregular pancake and egg-white shapes.

### 4.7 Clear liquids: Encapso K

A clear two-part encapsulation rubber for **lemonade, martinis, milk, orange juice, piña coladas, and broth**.
- **Mix:** 1A:1B by volume. **Pot life 2 hours. Full cure 24 hours.** Don't move the glass until it has cured.
- Cures to a soft rubber like a bouncy ball. Clear as water, resists UV, lasts 5+ years.
- **Tint** with Silc Pig or Ignite (fluorescent) silicone pigments, **a drop at a time** from a medicine dropper:
  - White milk: 2 drops white.
  - Chocolate milk: 1 drop white + ½ drop brown.
  - Orange juice: red + yellow + white.
  - Pink lemonade: 1 drop Ignite Fluorescent Pink.
  - Broth: a tiny amount of yellow.
- **Shake Part A vigorously. Do not shake Part B**, because shaking adds bubbles.
- Wear **vinyl gloves**; latex stops it curing. Keep it off skin and work with ventilation.
- **Seal any painted inclusions** (olives, for example) with Premiere Clear or acrylic gloss first. Unsealed paint can slow or stop the cure.
- **Anchor inclusions**, or they float up during the 2-hour pot life. Fix the olive pick to the glass. Let wontons and paper scallions float and push them down as it cures.
- **Layering ice.** Pour the first batch half-full and wait about 90 minutes for it to start setting. Then place the acrylic ice cubes, wait about 10 minutes, and pour the second batch.
- **Wood shavings** stirred in read as coconut pulp (piña colada).
- **Reusing glassware.** Coat the glass with petroleum jelly or Ease Release 200 first. Cured Encapso K can then be cut or crumbled out.
- **Crushed effects.** Cured Encapso K can be shaved or crumbled to look like broken glass, ice, or diamonds.
- **Cheaper hard alternative:** the FloralCraft Acrylic Water Kit, a two-part clear gel that sets hard in 24–48 hours.

### 4.9 Glazes, stains, and sealers

**Zinsser Bulls Eye shellac** (amber or clear)
- Amber is the default "baked" colour for crusts, muffins, egg-white edges, and fried egg rolls.
- **Dilute 1 shellac : 2 denatured alcohol** and build it up in thin glazes. You get the colour without the thick "encapsulated" plastic look.
- Use it as a sealer over salt dough and painted pieces.
- Seal wax with clear shellac before adding foam.
- Repeated coats harden a pie enough to survive a run.
- Dries fast. Flammable. Cleans up with denatured alcohol (or ammonia and water).

**FEV (French Enamel Varnish)**
- **1 part shellac : 10 parts denatured alcohol, plus leather dye** (brown and red) to colour. Clear or amber shellac makes no difference.
- Completely transparent, deep colour, soaks into crevices, dries in 15–30 minutes, durable.
- Used for **bacon, ham rind, and turkey skin**.

**Design Master Glossy Wood Tone spray** is the "oven browning" finish. Mist it where food would brown: crust edges, pancake rims, cake edges against the pan, meat ends. It also tones lettuce, cherries, and fabric. Leave some areas bare for variation.

**Other finishes**
- **Montana GOLD spray (NC-acrylic lacquer).** Fast-drying solvent lacquer, good on porous surfaces. Cream for doughnuts. Smash Potato (light yellow) for pancakes and cake; it was discontinued, so use GOLD Vanilla or BLACK "Smash 137".
- **Minwax Wood Finish stain.** Special Walnut on sawdust for peppered salami; English Chestnut with FEV on turkey. Dries in 4–6 hours.
- **India ink** (Dr. Ph. Martin's Bombay) and concentrated liquid watercolour.
  - Yellow ink in acrylic gloss = **popcorn butter**.
  - Brown ink in water = **dye bath for noodles, chicken, and paper vegetables** (2 drops per 2 cups of water; test first).
  - Watercolour + Elmer's School Glue Gel = a cheaper syrup.

**Sealers by sheen**

| Sheen | Product |
|---|---|
| Wet / glossy | Rosco Clear Acrylic **Gloss** |
| Satin (vegetables) | Mix Gloss + Flat |
| Matte / dry (cured salami) | Rosco Clear Acrylic **Flat** |
| Maximum durability | **Rosco Premiere Clear** water-based polyurethane (Gloss, Satin, Flat). Hard, abrasion-resistant, low VOC. Used on bread, wings, noodles, turkey, olives |

**Acrylic medium + paint = a transparent glaze.** You can also use thinned CrystalGel, Sculpt or Coat, Clear Acrylic, or Plastic Varnish as the medium. Example: burnt umber + medium settled into the crevices of a sticky bun for cinnamon.

**Paints.** Rosco **Off Broadway** and **Supersaturated** (concentrated vinyl acrylics, about $100 for full 1-oz kits) and Sculptural Arts **Artist's Choice** (dilutes up to 20:1). Leftover latex house paint and craft acrylics also work.
- With black, white, and the three primaries plus colour-mixing knowledge, you can mix anything.
- **Reds cover poorly.** Plan on 2–3 coats over white clay.

## 6. Finishing workflow: making it read as food

A repeatable order of operations, distilled from every recipe:

1. **Form.** Carve, cast, sculpt, or pour.
2. **Seal or prime.** FoamCoat, FlexCoat, CrystalGel, or FlexBond on foam. Shellac on salt dough. Krylon Crystal Clear on organic originals before molding.
3. **Base colour.** The palest colour of the food, matched to reference. Pink XPS can skip this for meats.
4. **Texture layer** if needed: sawdust, latex, papier-mâché, caulk.
5. **Transparent glaze layers.** Amber shellac (diluted), FEV, tinted CrystalGel, ink, or acrylic medium glazes. Build depth in thin coats.
6. **Browning and toning.** Glossy Wood Tone mist, or sponged and stippled darker colour. Don't over-sponge; it flattens texture into "visual mud".
7. **Detail.** Char on bone ends, dark shadows in joints and crevices, intensified colour at shrimp-tail tips.
8. **Final sheen.** Gloss for wet, satin for vegetables, flat for dry cured meat, Premiere Clear for anything handled hard.
9. **Assemble and attach** with CrystalGel, Sculpt or Coat, hot glue (low-temp for cast parts), or melted gel wax.

## 7. Recipe cards

Format: **Time**, **Key materials**, **Method**, **Tips**, **Show use**. Times include cure or dry time where the book gives it.

### Section 2: Appetizers

#### 4. Breads: baguette, sourdough round, half rustic round, rye loaf
- **Time:** 3 hr each for the XPS loaves. Rye: 1 hr prep + 8 hr cure.
- **Baguette:** Laminate three 1" XPS strips (3½" × 22") with Titebond II, clamp, and wait 1 hr. Surform to a rounded, tapered loaf. Carve diagonal slashes every ~2" so they look scored and puffed. Sand → FlexCoat → off-white base (white + raw or earth umber) → even amber shellac → Wood Tone where browned → Premiere Clear Gloss.
- **Sourdough:** Laminate three 10"-diameter discs (3" thick). Carve a centre valley as the scored "ear". Finish as for the baguette.
- **Half rustic round:** Cut two 10" discs in half, laminate the four half-discs with Fastbond, and wait 1 hr. Carve an undulating top so it reads as a round cut in half.
  - FlexCoat → base. **Sprinkle sawdust into the wet base** for crust. Lightly sponge a darker tan (white + raw sienna + umber). Don't overdo it.
  - Wood Tone, then sparing amber shellac.
- **Rye:** FlexFoam III (1A:2B) with Silc Pig brown, poured into a loaf pan (Teflon needs no release; otherwise use Ease Release 2831). **Cover with wax paper** for a pocked crust; let the top collapse for irregularity. Demold → amber shellac → sawdust → second amber coat → uneven Wood Tone.

#### 5. Cheese platter: brie, cheddar, Swiss, gouda
- **Time:** 10 hr work, 24 hr dry. All cast in ceramic dishes or cake pans sprayed with Universal Mold Release.
- **Brie:** Melt a 4" gel block + 2" creme block with yellow dye. Pour, set, demold. Coat with white Alex Plus caulk smoothed with wet gloved fingers for the rind and cure overnight. Cut wedges with a hot knife on wax paper.
- **Cheddar:** 1 gel : 1 creme with yellow + red dye. Hot-knife wedges.
- **Swiss:** 2 gel : 1 creme with white + yellow dye, set in a metal cake pan for about 2 hr. It's soft enough for an ordinary knife. Drill holes with several bit sizes (start with ⅝").
- **Gouda:** 1 gel : 1 creme with yellow + red. Liquid-latex rind toned with Wood Tone.
- **Tip:** Store-bought fake cheese is expensive and seldom the right form.
- **Show use:** *Don Giovanni* banquet (CCM); actors mimed eating.

#### 6. Chicken wings
- **Time:** About 8 hr (6 hr mold cure, 15 min per casting, 90 min painting).
- **Method:** Wash off sauce. Seal real wings with Krylon Crystal Clear or shellac. Hot-glue them into a foam-core box, release with Ease Release 200, and pour **OOMOO 30** (8 oz is enough). Cure 6 hr and demold with relief cuts. Tape the mold closed with medical tape. Cast **Smooth-Cast 300** (3 min pot life, demold at 15 min).
  - Paint: Fire Red, Orange, Deep Red, Burnt Sienna. Topcoat with Premiere Clear Gloss.
- **Tip:** Silicone beats plaster. Plaster breaks if dropped; OOMOO resists tearing.
- **Show use:** Fried chicken for *Assassins* (see the Fake 'n Bake blog for a plaster-mold method).

#### 7. Egg rolls, spring rolls, soy sauce
- **Time:** 1.5 hr + 24 hr dry.
- **Egg rolls:** Stovetop salt dough (V2), rolled under ⅛", cut into 4½" squares. Lay a log of Creative Paperclay filling across and roll like a real egg roll. Brush with amber shellac and **seal in a zip bag for 24 hr** to get the granular fried skin.
- **Spring rolls:** White Thai Unryu paper squares wrapped around Paperclay plus slivers of green and orange Unryu. Finish with a thin coat of clear shellac.
- **Soy sauce:** Gel wax + a little brown dye (or a brown crayon), poured into the dish.

#### 8. Oysters on the half shell
- **Time:** 2 hr + overnight cure.
- **Method:** Clean the shells. If you shuck real oysters, use an oyster knife, hold the shell in a towel or glove, pry at the hinge, then discard the meat and clean the shells thoroughly.
  - Body: **2 parts liquid latex : 1 part Sculpt or Coat**, tinted Neutral Tone Gray.
  - Adductor muscle: a teaspoon dollop of acrylic gloss medium + gray + black.
  - Mantle edge: Sculpt or Coat + Masking Black **piped with a glue syringe**.
- **Tip:** A clipped zip-bag corner works for piping but takes practice to get an even bead.
- **Show use:** *Don Giovanni* banquet.

#### 9. Popcorn
- **Time:** 30 min.
- **Method:** Wedge an XPS block into the container so you don't have to fill it. Break S-shaped packing peanuts into popcorn-sized pieces and heap them above the rim. Flood with FlexBond (or Elmer's) to fix and prime them; let it set before moving. Paint neutral beige. Wearing gloves, pour yellow India ink mixed into acrylic gloss over the top and spread it with a finger.
- **Tips:** Doesn't attract pests. Peanuts give static shocks. Crescent-shaped peanut pieces work as cashews.
- **Show use:** *The Rocky Horror Show*; *If You Take a Mouse to the Movies* (scale up for the style).

#### 10. Shrimp cocktail
- **Time:** 8 hr (15 min per shrimp mold) + 24 hr.
- **Method:** Seal a frozen shrimp with Krylon, glue it into a box, and pour **OOMOO 25** (15 min working time, demold at 75 min). Slit the side to free the crescent shape and tape it closed. Cast Smooth-Cast 300 from a 3-oz cup.
  - Paint with acrylics, intensifying the colour at the tail tips. Clear nail polish adds shine, and **glossy Scotch tape on the tail end** fakes the translucent shell.
- **Cocktail sauce:** Gel wax + red dye + fine wood shavings for horseradish, poured into a martini glass. Glue the shrimp to the rim.

#### 11. Vegetable tray
- **Time:** 4–8 hr.
- **Cherry tomatoes:** Model Magic balls from nickel to quarter size. Dry 2 hr. Paint 2–3 coats of Fire Red with a cotton swab, then CrystalGel.
- **Baby carrots:** 2" Model Magic ropes in varied thicknesses, one end tapered. Paint Orange, then gloss for a moist look.
- **Green beans:** Pencil-sized ropes, dimpled to suggest beans in the pod, with a seam pressed in with a **metal ruler** edge and tapered ends. Paint yellow ochre + chrome oxide green + a little umber. Satin finish (gloss + flat).
- **Broccoli:** 1" lengths of fringed trim; the selvage becomes the stalk. Hot-glue 3 or more together. Dab chrome oxide green on the fringe.
- **Lettuce bed:** Cut ~5" leaves from 5–6 stacked Bounty towels. Coat each with CrystalGel by hand until at least 50% saturated, scrunch for veining, unfold, cup slightly on wax paper, and dry 30 min. Paint with greens and yellows. **Too much water dissolves the CrystalGel** and the leaves go flat.
- (A fruit-tray recipe is on the companion website.)

#### 12. Wonton soup
- **Time:** 35 min + 24 hr cure.
- **Method:** Make low-salt wonton dough and cut 4" squares. Filling per wonton: 1 tbsp FlexBond + 1 tbsp sawdust + 2 tsp Van Dyke Brown. Fold into a triangle-and-tuck or four-corner gather and press out the air.
  - Wrap in a damp towel and microwave 15 s. Re-wet and microwave 30 s more to get the boiled sheen.
  - Broth: Encapso K (8–10 oz) + a tiny drop of yellow Silc Pig, poured over the wontons, which float. Push them down as it cures. Add green Unryu scallions while it's still liquid.

### Section 3: Breakfast

#### 13. Doughnuts
- **Time:** 3 hr.
- **Chocolate cake doughnuts:** FlexFoam III + Silc Pig brown in a Wilton doughnut pan. The pockmarked surface reads as chocolate cake. Glaze with a thin coat of CrystalGel.
- **Plain cake doughnuts:** FlexFoam V. Fill cavities only part-full; it expands 11×. Demold at 2 hr. Spray with Montana GOLD Cream, then Glossy Wood Tone.
- **Chocolate frosted:** Alex Plus caulk + Sculpt or Coat + raw umber + black. Optional wood shavings as toasted coconut. Dries in about 30 min.
- **Release:** Ease Release 2831 (or Universal then 2831); none needed for Teflon.
- **Show use:** *Superior Donuts*; diner shows (*Bus Stop*, *Spitfire Grill*).

#### 14. Oatmeal muffins
- **Time:** 3 hr.
- **Method:** FlexFoam V in a Wilton muffin pan with release. Demold at 40 min. Glaze with amber shellac diluted 1:2 in alcohol, building up layers. Top with **instant oatmeal mixed into CrystalGel or FlexBond**, then more shellac and a Wood Tone dusting.
- **Show use:** *Into the Woods* (CCM); diner shows. (Poppy-seed muffins are on the companion website.)

#### 15. Pancake, bacon, and egg breakfast
- **Time:** 5 hr.
- **Over-easy eggs:**
  - White: FlexFoam III spooned into a released 6" pan in an irregular shape. Brush amber shellac along the edge as the crispy fringe.
  - **Seal the white well with clear shellac.**
  - Yolk: yellow gel wax poured less than halfway into a released plastic egg half; the freezer speeds it up.
  - Attach the yolk with Sculpt or Coat, CrystalGel, or caulk (the first two dry clear). Pour a thin film of white-tinted gel wax over the top as the cooked white membrane.
- **Pancakes:** FlexFoam III (a quarter of a 3-oz cup of A + half a cup of B makes one pancake) spooned into released 6" pans. Spray light yellow (Montana Smash Potato or a substitute) plus brown, with Wood Tone on the rims.
  - **Butter:** Adapt-it or Polly Plastics, painted yellow ochre + white.
  - **Syrup:** Gel wax + brown dye, or **Elmer's School Glue Gel + Dr. Ph. Martin's watercolour** for less money.
- **Bacon:** Cut strips of 4–6 mil plastic sheeting (thicker is better) and heat-gun them slowly from a distance until they pucker and curl. Coat with caulk, then layer red and brown **FEV**.
- **Show use:** Diner shows; reuse for *Diner* and *Waitress*.

#### 16. Sticky buns
- **Time:** 3 hr.
- **Mold:** Hot-glue a plastic bread roll into a well-sealed foam-core box. Pour **Dragon Skin 10 Fast** (1:1, stir 3 min, ~8 min working time) at the lowest point to ½" over the top. Cure 75 min. Use vinyl gloves only.
- **Cast:** Brush on Ease Release 2831 and let it flash off 10 min. Pour FlexFoam V.
- **Finish:** Diluted amber shellac glazes → Wood Tone. Burnt umber + acrylic medium in the knot crevices for cinnamon. **Melted vanilla soy candle beads** drizzled as icing, pooling where real icing would. Hot-glue jewellery beads on as chopped nuts.
- **Show use:** *Into the Woods* (CCM); diner shows.

### Section 4: Entrées and sides

#### 17. Baked potato with butter, sour cream, and cheese
- **Time:** 8 hr.
- **Method:** Laminate three ¾" XPS sheets (6" × 12") with Elmer's or Fastbond, clamped well; one block yields two potatoes. Cut the outlines on a band saw, then shape with a Surform or Dremel. Seal with Elmer's, FlexBond, or FoamCoat.
  - **Glue on brown paper towel as skin** and paint it burnt and raw umber. For a salted skin, sprinkle PVC shavings or large white glitter into glue or CrystalGel.
  - Cut a V-notch for the opening, then FoamCoat and paint the flesh.
- **Toppings:**
  - Sour cream: 2 tbsp CrystalGel + white.
  - Butter: Polly Plastics, 6–10 white + 1 yellow pellet. Glaze white if it's too yellow.
  - Shredded cheddar: grated creme wax (pour at 160–170°F).

#### 18. Barbecue ribs with corn on the cob
- **Time:** 7 hr.
- **Corn:** Clean all silk off a real ear. Seal it with Krylon, box it (8" × 3" × 6"), and pour OOMOO 30 at least 1" over the top. Cure 6 hr. Keep relief cuts minimal.
  - Cast in **creme wax tinted with golden yellow, yellow, and brown crayon**. Don't move the mold for 30 or more minutes.
- **Ribs:** Seal a cleaned rib bone and mold it in OOMOO 30. Release twice. Cast Smooth-Cast 300 tinted with a smidge of Silc Pig flesh tone; a slab has about 14 bones.
  - Meat: eight 1" × 4" × 1" XPS pieces, rasped, with Dremel cavities. Glue the bones in with low-temp hot glue.
  - Liquid latex (Kangaroo) for meat texture, then **CrystalGel tinted burnt sienna + fire red + deep red, about ¼" thick**, as the bark or sauce. Char the bone ends, mist with Wood Tone, and finish with clear acrylic gloss.

#### 19. Chef salad
- **Time:** 3½–4 hr.
- **Lettuce:** Find a leaf template online (e.g. "lettuce leaf template") and enlarge it to about 7" × 9". Trace onto Cottonelle folded 3–4 times; its ribs mimic veins. Cut with scissors. Saturate fully with CrystalGel, pinch a centre rib, and dry 30 min (a hair dryer speeds it up).
  - Paint with greens and yellows mixed with some CrystalGel and a little water so it stays translucent without dissolving. Make 1–1½ cups of leaves, plus extras.
- **Meat:** Strips of silk lining (turkey) and the back of pink corduroy (ham).
- **Tomatoes:** Model Magic, as in Ch. 11.
- **Cheese:** Grated creme cheddar.
- **Hard-boiled eggs:** Pour white-crayon-tinted gel wax into Easter-egg halves held upright in a muffin tin. Sink Model Magic yolk balls into the warm wax. Pop out when set and slice with a hot or heated blade.

#### 20. Chicken lo mein
- **Time:** 20 min + overnight to 24 hr dry.
- **Noodles:** 12–16" lengths of cotton cord or yarn dipped in batches of 20–25 into **black tea** (tea worked better) or 2 drops of brown India ink per 2 cups of water. Wring out or squeeze in a zip bag and dry 15 min.
- **Chicken:** ⅛" Model Magic strips glazed tan (white + yellow ochre in Premiere Clear), then a dilute ink wash.
- **Vegetables:** Green and orange Unryu paper dipped in the same bath.
- **Finish:** Pour 1:1 Premiere Clear Gloss and water over everything and dry 24 hr.
- (General Tso's/sesame chicken and fried rice are on the companion website.)

#### 21. Club sandwich
- **Time:** 6 hr. Materials cost about $10.50 (⅛ yd fabric, 1 yd trim, scrap foam).
- **Bread:** 1" upholstery foam split to ½", cut into 4" squares, then halved diagonally (six triangles per sandwich). Dip in thinned off-white, paint the crust edges light brown, and toast with a Wood Tone dusting.
- **Fillings:**
  - 4" circles of pink corduroy (ham) and silk (turkey), folded loosely.
  - Green ruffle trim (lettuce).
  - Ruffle-trim strips painted as bacon (fat: white + burnt umber + yellow ochre; meat: burnt sienna + umber).
  - Tomato slices: ¼" of 1:1 gel and creme wax tinted with red crayon, cast in a ramekin and halved.
- **Glue:** CrystalGel + white + yellow ochre **mayonnaise**. Build the stack upside-down, flip it, and add a frill pick.

#### 22. Hanging meats: salami, peppered salami, sausage
- **Time:** 5–8 hr.
- **Salami:** Laminate three 1" XPS strips with Titebond, using cauls under the clamps. Cartoon the shape, cut it, and shape with a sander. Prime if it'll be handled.
  - Paint the casing earth umber + raw sienna + red, then acrylic gloss.
  - **For a moulded casing, dust baby powder onto the wet gloss.**
  - Seal with Premiere Clear or acrylic Flat. Slide it into a 24" length of meat netting and tie the ends with twine.
  - Variant without mould: liquid latex before painting.
- **Peppered salami:** Earth umber base → thin FlexBond → roll in sawdust → Minwax Special Walnut stain (4–6 hr) → Wood Tone + light gray mist. Faster version: tone with Wood Tone + black spray. Seal.
  - For **sliced medallions**, paint the cut faces with Americana Red Barn thinned 1:1.
- **Sausage:** A 14" length of backer rod bent into a loop, plus a 9" link. FlexCoat → raw sienna + earth umber → Wood Tone → gloss.

#### 23. Honey-glazed ham
- **Time:** 4 hr.
- **Method:** Laminate three 5" × 6" × 2" XPS pieces with Fastbond into a 6" × 5" × 6" block. Shape with a band saw and pull saw. Drill a 1" hole and hot-glue in a stub of 1" PVC (2" long or less) as the bone.
  - Burnt sienna base → CrystalGel seal → **Dremel engraving-bit cross-hatch** for scoring → more CrystalGel.
  - **Two coats of liquid latex as fat**, with a blob over the bone end.
  - FEV the rind. Brown-sugar glaze: **sawdust in CrystalGel**, then Wood Tone.
  - Cut face: brilliant red, burnt sienna, white, and neutral, then amber shellac and CrystalGel.
- **Tip:** Starting from pink foam saves a base coat. Layered translucent glazes are what sell it.

#### 24. Lettuce wraps
- **Time:** 2–3 hr.
- **Method:** Bounty leaves balled up in CrystalGel, cupped, and painted as in Ch. 11. If the first CrystalGel coat isn't fully cured (24 hr), water in the paint will flatten them.
  - Filling: crumbled packing peanuts mixed with Rosco RoBo glue or Elmer's, spooned into a muffin tin to set six mounds at once. Glaze with raw umber + acrylic gloss.
  - Wrap with CrystalGel as the adhesive.

#### 25. Lobster tail
- **Time:** 2 hr (24–72 hr full cure).
- **Method:** Cut the head off a hollow plastic lobster and split the tail lengthwise. Strip the inside but keep the fins, and sand the edges with a Dremel. Press in ~2 oz Model Magic, set 30 min, and ease it out.
  - **Shell:** Scumble fire red + orange + burnt sienna, stipple brilliant red, and shadow the joints with burnt sienna. Gloss.
  - **Meat:** Press a second piece and trim off the fin. At about 45 min, while still pliable, **split the top and push it together** like a cooked split-top. Stipple and dry-brush fire red and orange on the ridges.
- **Show use:** *Tribes* (Nina Raine).

#### 26. Pierogi
- **Time:** 30 min + overnight dry.
- **Method:** Low-salt stovetop dough (about 8 min on the stove), rolled very thin. Cut 4" circles, add a 1" dough ball as filling, fold, and crimp with a scalloped clay tool or by hand.
  - Air-dry overnight (or microwave 15 s). Clear shellac, then burnt sienna for the pan-seared spots.
- **Show use:** *The Three Sisters*.

#### 27. Roast beef with mashed potatoes and gravy
- **Time:** 8 hr.
- **Roast:** Five laminated 4" × 6" layers of pink XPS, shaped (here, a recycled foam ham). Slice ¼–½" thick with a hacksaw and **rasp the faces** for meat texture, which also hides the glue lines.
  - Paint the edges with full-strength earth umber (seared). Interior: an earth umber wash over pink, applied by loading the brush and then dipping it in water. Finish with a CrystalGel sheen.
- **Mashed potatoes:** Stovetop salt dough (no alum) spooned onto wax paper and ridged with a spoon. White wash, then acrylic gloss.
- **Gravy:** 1 cup gel wax + brown crayon, test-poured, then ladled over.

#### 28. Salmon fillet with side salad
- **Time:** 3 hr.
- **Salmon:** Sculpt a 2" × 4" fillet from Creative Paperclay on plastic, with flake grain from a sculpting tool. It isn't a neat block. Dry overnight or 30 min at 250°F. Paint light coral, pink, and dark coral. Clear shellac.
- **Salad:** Tissue paper in three greens (plus purple for cabbage) dipped in Sculpt or Coat, rolled, and pulled apart.
  - Tone with Wood Tone + almond spray.
  - Slivered orange silk-poppy petals as carrot.
  - Diced tomato: 2 gel : 1 creme + red with **white seed beads** as seeds, set in any pan and cubed.
  - Sliced almonds from balsa with umber edges.
  - Fix it all with Sculpt or Coat, or with CrystalGel for a dressed sheen.
- **Show use:** *Don Giovanni* banquet.

#### 29. Spinach quiche
- **Time:** 3 hr.
- **Method:** Salt dough pressed into a tart pan sprayed with PAM, baked at 350°F for 20 min, then amber shellac.
  - Spinach: torn green Unryu paper (sprayed hunter green if needed). Pale-yellow paper for onion and cheese.
  - Custard: gel wax (2 gel : 1 creme for a softer set) + yellow + white. Pour it in and push the "spinach" partly under.
  - Once cool, glaze the top with amber shellac to suggest browning and oils.

#### 30. Roast turkey
- **Time:** 30–42 hr, mostly lamination cure.
- **Body:** Five 12" × 18" × 1" XPS layers with Titebond, weighted, 24–36 hr. Cut an elliptical dome and Surform and sand it.
- **Legs and wings:** Two 2"-thick 6" × 12" laminations. Cartoon from a template, band-saw, and shape. Carve troughs so the wings seat. Glue with Titebond or FlexBond and **pin with finish nails** while curing, then pull the nails and re-blend.
- **Skin:**
  - Papier-mâché torn brown paper towel (no factory edges) with 2 FlexBond : 1 water. This blends the joints and the skin webbing.
  - Two coats of liquid latex.
  - Tan base (Valspar Touch of Tan, or white + raw sienna + earth umber) with sawdust sprinkled into the wet paint.
  - **FEV + Minwax English Chestnut**, then a Wood Tone mist.
  - Premiere Clear Gloss.
- (A large Christmas goose is on the companion website.)
- **Show use:** *A Christmas Carol*, *You Can't Take It with You*, any feast.

### Section 5: Beverages

#### 31. Beer
- **Time:** 2 hr.
- **Method:** Melt a 6" gel block with gold dye. **Heat to about 215°F for bubbles** and pour into the mug. Once set, **seal the surface with clear shellac or Premiere Clear**; otherwise the dye leaches into the foam head.
  - Head: less than 1 tbsp of FlexFoam V (1:1, measured by cup ridges) poured on top. Cures in 2 hr.

#### 32. Hot chocolate
- **Time:** 1 hr.
- **Method:** A 4" gel block + brown and white dye poured into the mug. **Marshmallows:** small Model Magic balls flattened on two sides, some sunk and some floating.
- **Durability:** Still looked new after 5+ years.

#### 33. Irish coffee
- **Time:** 3 hr.
- **Method:** A 4" gel block + half a brown dye block poured into the mug. The glass gets very hot. When cool, seal with clear shellac. **Whipped cream:** FlexFoam III (1:2) mixed with a craft stick and plopped on top.

#### 34. Lemonade (pink)
- **Time:** 1.5 hr + 24 hr cure.
- **Method:** Encapso K with one drop of Ignite Fluorescent Pink, from 1½ trial kits.
  - Fill the pitcher halfway and wait about 90 min. Add acrylic ice cubes, wait about 10 min, then top up with a second batch. Do the same for the glass.
  - Notch plastic lemon slices to sit on the rims, and re-texture the flat pulp with **Liquitex String Gel**.
- **Show use:** *Picnic* porch dressing.

#### 35. Martini with olives
- **Time:** 45 min + 24 hr.
- **Method:** Penny-sized Model Magic olives on a pick, painted ultramarine + yellow ochre + chrome oxide green, with an optional red pimento dab. **Seal with gloss before pouring**, or the Encapso K may not cure.
  - **Fix the pick to the glass**, then pour clear Encapso K.
- **Alternative:** FloralCraft Acrylic Water Kit.

#### 36. Milk (white and chocolate)
- **Time:** 10 min + 24 hr.
- **Method:** Encapso K. White milk takes 2 drops of white Silc Pig. Chocolate milk takes 1 drop white + ½ drop brown. Orange juice takes red + yellow + white.
- **Show use:** Diner shows; *Hay Fever*.

#### 37. Piña colada
- **Time:** 2 hr + 24 hr.
- **Method:** Encapso K + 2 drops white + wood shavings crushed in a bag as coconut, poured into a poco grande glass and cured 24 hr. Froth: FlexFoam V on top; leave room for 11× expansion. Artificial cherries misted with Wood Tone, resting against the rim.

### Section 6: Desserts

#### 38. Apple tart
- **Time:** 90 min work, 12–18 hr total.
- **Crust:** V3 salt dough rolled to an ⅛"-thick, 14" circle and pressed into a fluted tart pan sprayed with PAM and floured. **Bake at 200°F for 60 min** so it doesn't puff. Amber shellac, flip, and bake the underside 5–10 min on the inverted pan. Shellac the bottom, adding extra where it would brown.
- **Filling:** A primed ½" XPS disc (trace a 9" cake pan) glued in as the base.
  - **Apple slices:** Model Magic rolled ¼" thick, cut with a 2" crescent cookie cutter, edges varied, air-dried overnight.
  - Glue them on, then paint yellow ochre + white + a little earth umber. Amber shellac on the edges, then Wood Tone.
- **Caramel:** Two 2" gel blocks + about 4 yellow and 2 brown crayon shavings, drizzled so it pools in places and drips onto the crust.

#### 39. Cake with a removable edible piece
- **Time:** 2.5 hr + 48 hr dry.
- **Fake cake:** Three 9" × 13" × 1" XPS sheets (a quarter-sheet cake) laminated with Titebond and weighted. Trim, then round the edges with a sander. **Cut away the section that will be eaten** (a corner works well). Prime, paint cake colour, and seal with shellac.
- **Food-safety barrier:** Wrap every surface the edible piece will touch in **plastic wrap, overlapping the edges**. Bulls Eye shellac is marketed as food-safe, but don't rely on it.
- **Fake icing:** **Equal parts CrystalGel and Swans Down cake flour** plus paint. Cake flour is finer and lower in protein than all-purpose, so it reads more like icing. Spread ¼–½" thick with an icing spatula and **feather it thin near the edible piece**. Cure overnight.
- **Edible piece:** Thawed Sara Lee pound cake cut to fit (3" × 2½" × 3" here). Spread canned frosting on the plastic-wrapped faces, set the piece in, and blend real frosting into fake, piping into the seam if needed.
- **Decoration:** Pipe stars (bag perpendicular, squeeze, then lift straight up for a peak) or a rope border (star tip at about 45°, gentle nested "S" curves) with the leftover fake icing.
- **Show use:** *Crimes of the Heart*.

#### 40. Cherry pie
- **Time:** 4 hr.
- **Crust:** Salt dough in a pie pan sprayed with PAM, fluted every 2" with a thumb-and-finger pinch. **Bake at 250°F for 15 min.** Shellac, flip, and bake the underside 15 min. Shellac again.
- **Filling:** A 4" gel block (or 1:1 gel and creme) + cranberry and red dye, with **round plastic grapes** as cherries, some submerged and some in clusters.
- **Lattice:** ½" salt dough strips woven over the warm filling, then baked at 350°F for 15–20 min until white.
- **Finish:** Amber shellac diluted 1:2, built up in coats until the pie is hard enough for the run, plus Wood Tone on the crust edge.
- **Show use:** *Into the Woods* (CCM). (Mini berry pies are on the companion website.)

#### 41. Chocolate cake à la mode
- **Time:** 1 hr + overnight.
- **Cake:** Two isosceles XPS triangles (2" × 6", from 1" sheet), with the short edge slightly curved like a slice from a round cake. Paint burnt umber.
- **Icing:** **3 parts CrystalGel : 2 parts burnt umber + Van Dyke Brown.** Make twice what you think you need. Ice the bottom layer, wait about 3 min, and stack gently. Ice the top and back, using the scalloped side of the spatula on the back. Dry overnight and scrape off any oozing. Pipe stars.
- **Ice cream:** A tennis-ball-sized ball of white Model Magic pressed into a **real lever-and-sweeper scoop** for a fresh-scooped texture. Attach with CrystalGel.
- **Drizzle:** Icing piped from a clipped zip bag.
- **Show use:** Diner shows.

#### 42. Coconut cream pie
- **Time:** 4 hr.
- **Crust:** Salt dough with a scalloped edge pressed in with a spoon. 350°F for 25 min, then amber shellac.
- **Filling:** A 4" gel block + white + a little brown, with **wood shavings stirred in** as coconut.
- **Topping:** Alex Plus caulk spread with a knife dipped in water. **Toasted coconut:** chopped shavings spatter-painted with burnt sienna + water on plastic, pressed into the caulk while it's still wet. Wood Tone on the crust edge.
- **Slicing:** Wait at least 2 hr after the caulk, then cut through caulk, wax, and crust with a sharp knife.

#### 43. Gelatin mold
- **Time:** 6 hr.
- **Method:** Spray a gelatin mold with Universal release. Pour gel + creme wax with red dye (check the temperature first) and let it set 3–4 hr. Invert onto a moistened plate, using a warm-water dip if it sticks.
  - **Whipped-cream stars:** Alex Plus caulk in a bag with a star tip. Roll the bag top down so it doesn't burst, squeeze, and pull up as you release.
- **Show use:** *Picnic*; jelly molds and flummery for *A Christmas Carol*.

#### 44. Pineapple upside-down cake
- **Time:** 4 hr.
- **Cake:** FlexFoam III in a 9" pan (Universal then Ease Release 2831, dried 10–30 min). Spray light yellow with Wood Tone on the edges.
- **Pineapple rings:** Two dried pineapple rings sealed with Krylon and molded in OOMOO 25 (demold at 75 min). Cast **Smooth-Cast 320** + a drop of yellow Silc Pig. It gets hot; demold at 15 min.
- **Cherries:** 1" Model Magic cubes rolled into balls with a mechanical-pencil dimple, painted medium red and red-orange. Red Model Magic would skip the painting.
- **Assembly:** Stick the fruit on with melted gel wax. Brush amber shellac around the fruit as caramelisation. Pour on a brown-sugar sauce of a 2" gel cube + a little brown dye.

## 8. Tool and supply list by tier

### Basic (grocery, craft, and discount stores)
- **Measuring:** 12–18" cork-backed metal ruler (it also cuts clay); locking steel tape; cloth tape; measuring cups (label them).
- **Marking:** Pencils (always scarce), Sharpies.
- **Cutting:** X-acto with #11 blades (change them when dull); snap-off and utility knives (full extension cuts foam); steak knives (rigid enough to carve foam); scissors (keep a pair just for fabric); self-healing cutting mats (11" × 17" and 24" × 36").
- **Clay and dough:** Crayola Model Magic; salt dough ingredients; cheap crayons for tinting wax.
- **Adhesives:** Hot glue gun (low and high temp); Elmer's Glue-All.
- **Paint:** Acrylics (black, white, red, yellow, blue minimum); chip brushes (2–4"); detail brush set (under $20).
- **Consumables:** Sandpaper (80, 150, 220); wax paper; plastic wrap; cotton swabs; Bounty towels; Cottonelle; 8-oz clear plastic cups with ridges (for measuring two-part products).
- **Dedicated kitchenware (fake food only, labelled):** Saucepan, spatulas, spoons, cheese grater; tongue depressors and jumbo craft sticks; tweezers.

### Intermediate (hardware stores, specialty craft, online)
- **Cutting and shaping:** Japanese Ryoba pull saw; orbital or palm sander; jig or sabre saw (clamp the work; foam up to ~3"); Surform shaver or pocket plane; cookie cutters; C-clamps (4–6") and Quick-Grip bar clamps (12" or longer); lauan or plywood cauls.
- **Decorating:** Cake decorating turntable; candle thermometer; adhesive (tile) spreader for ribbed frosting.
- **Adhesives:** Titebond wood glue.
- **Wax and plastics:** Yaley Gel Wax and Creme Wax plus concentrated dyes; Polly Plastics pellets.
- **Colour and finish:** India ink and concentrated watercolour; Bulls Eye shellac (amber and clear); Design Master Glossy Wood Tone (and the Colortool line, which is safe on Styrofoam).
- **Foams and caulk:** Flexible upholstery foam; XPS insulation board; DAP Alex Plus caulk.

### Advanced (theatrical suppliers such as Rose Brand and Production Advantage, Smooth-On dealers)
- **Power tools:** Band saw; Dremel with engraving, sanding, and cutting bits; heat gun.
- **Adhesives and coatings:** 3M Fastbond 30NF or DAP Weldwood water-based contact cement; liquid latex (an ammonia-free kind is less irritating).
- **Rosco:** CrystalGel, FlexBond, FoamCoat, FlexCoat.
- **Sculptural Arts:** Sculpt or Coat (about $30/qt in 2018).
- **Paints:** Rosco Off Broadway or Supersaturated kits (under $100); Artist's Choice sampler (under $100); Rosco Clear Acrylic Gloss and Flat; Rosco Premiere Clear.
- **Smooth-On trial kits:** OOMOO 25 and 30, Dragon Skin 10 Fast, Smooth-Cast 300 and 320, FlexFoam-iT! III and V, Encapso K, Silc Pig and Ignite pigments, Universal Mold Release, Ease Release 200 and 2831.

### Budget substitutions the book endorses
| Instead of | Use |
|---|---|

## 9. Troubleshooting and gotchas

| Problem | Cause → fix |
|---|---|
| Paint flakes off foam | No flexible primer → coat with FoamCoat, FlexCoat, CrystalGel, Sculpt or Coat, or FlexBond first |
| Foam melted under spray adhesive | New Super 77 contains acetone → use Super 77 Classic or 3M 78 Polystyrene, or water-based glues |
| Laminated foam slides or has voids | Not enough glue or pressure → spread glue over the whole face, clamp with cauls, or weight or ratchet-strap it |
| Clamps dented foam | No cauls → put lauan or plywood between the clamps and the foam |
| Urethane foam collapsed or shrivelled | Used silicone or Universal release alone → use Ease Release 2831 (or Universal then 2831) |
| Foam has a smooth plastic skin | Exposed surface cured smooth → cover the pour with wax paper |
| Foam overflowed the mold | Overfilled; it expands 11–15× → fill only partway |
| Foam won't mix in time | Pot life is 35–50 s → pre-measure, pre-mix Part B, have the mold ready, pour immediately |
| Dye bled from wax into foam | Wax not sealed → clear shellac or Premiere Clear over the wax before the foam goes on |
| Wax is cloudy, bubbly, or slow to set | Too hot, or gel only → keep it at 160–170°F; add creme for structure and fewer bubbles (unless you *want* bubbles, as in beer at 215°F) |
| Wax cheese crumbles or is hard to cut | Too much creme → 2:1 gel to creme; use a hot knife |
| Wax leaked out of a silicone mold | Relief cuts → minimise them; tape the mold closed with medical tape |
| Silicone mold failed or stuck to the original | Organic original not sealed → Krylon Crystal Clear, then Ease Release 200 |
| Mold box leaked | Seams not sealed → thick hot-glue beads plus tape on the outside |
| Mold tore on demold | Too thin over the high point → pour at least ½–1" over it |
| Taped mold opens during casting | Wrong tape → medical tape (masking and Scotch tape failed) |
| Dragon Skin or Encapso K won't cure | Latex gloves, or unsealed paint inside → vinyl gloves; seal inclusions with gloss |
| Encapso K full of bubbles | Part B was shaken → shake A only; stir B |
| Garnish floated to the top | Not anchored → fix the pick to the glass |
| Paper lettuce went flat | Too much water dissolved the CrystalGel, or the first coat hadn't cured (24 hr) → mix CrystalGel into the paint and use less water |
| Salt dough cracks while rolling | No oil or alum → use V1–V3 |
| Salt dough sticky | Cold-mixed → use the stovetop version |
| Salt dough puffed or bubbled | Baked too hot → 200–250°F for longer |
| Amber shellac looks plastic or encapsulated | Coats too thick → dilute 1:2 with alcohol and build thin glazes |
| Texture looks muddy | Over-sponged dark colour → sponge lightly and leave highlights |
| Reds look streaky over white | Reds cover poorly → 2–3 coats, or start with red Model Magic |
| Store-bought fake looks fake | Wrong sheen, colour, or texture → re-texture (String Gel), re-tone (Wood Tone), change the sheen |
| Spray nozzle clogs | Residue in the tip → invert and spray until clear after every use |
| Packing peanuts zap you | Static → expected. Ground yourself |
