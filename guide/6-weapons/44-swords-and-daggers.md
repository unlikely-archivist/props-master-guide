# 44. Swords, Daggers and Blades

> **Sources:** M ch.10 (p. 148); H ch.1 (pp. 3–4), ch.8 (pp. 158–160), ch.10 (pp. 190–191); KF (pp. 5, 10–23, 30, 46–47); J (arms entries). Sections marked **[General knowledge]** aren't from the guide's sources.
> **[Check current]** Laws on carrying, buying and transporting blades vary by place. Stage-combat standards are set by fight directors and their professional bodies; check current guidance.

Blades are the most common stage weapons and the most varied: from a foam sword carried in a procession to a steel blade in a choreographed fight. The first question is always **what the blade must do**: be worn, be drawn, be brandished, make contact in a fight, appear to cut, or break.

## Choose by use [General knowledge]

| Use | Typical prop | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Worn or carried only** | Wood, resin, EVA foam, 3D print, or a cheap decorative sword with the edge dulled | Weight and balance still matter to the actor ([ch. 22](../4-fabrication-core/22-the-build-method.md)) |
| **Drawn and brandished, no contact** | Light, dull, well-balanced blade; aluminium or wood for safety | Check that it draws smoothly from its scabbard every time |
| **Choreographed contact (a fight)** | **Purpose-made stage-combat weapon**, approved by the fight director | Never a decorative "wall hanger." See below |
| **Close-quarters or falling** | Rubber, latex-coated foam, or foam stunt version | Film stunt weapons are often single foam-rubber castings (H 190–191) |
| **Appears to cut or stab** | Trick blade: retractable, collapsible, or a blood knife | See [ch. 50](../7-effects/50-blood-and-messy-effects.md) and [ch. 46](../7-effects/46-effects-and-breakaways.md) |
| **Breaks on cue** | Rigged breakaway that reassembles | (H 3–4; [ch. 46](../7-effects/46-effects-and-breakaways.md#breakaways-h-34-h-77-190191-274275)) |

## Blades from the sources

- **Dull every blade and edge** used onstage: bench grinder, stone or metal sandpaper (M 148).
- **Stage-combat blades are spring steel** (for example 5160) so they flex instead of snapping (H 158).
- **Film combat blades in 7075 aluminium** read as steel on camera but dent too fast for a stage run (H 160).
- **A real cutting edge** (Hart's axe that really splits a melon nightly needs tool steel, H 159) demands a careful written safety plan.
- **Coordinate every fight weapon with the fight director**, or the stunt department on film (H 4).
- **Lock up all blades every night** (M 148–149).

## Stage-combat weapons [General knowledge]

- **Why decorative swords are dangerous.** Cheap display swords often have a thin "rat-tail" tang (the part of the blade inside the handle) welded on, and brittle blades. In a fight they can snap at the hilt and send the blade flying. **Never use a decorative sword for contact.**
- **What a combat weapon has**: a full or thick tang, a blade designed to flex and absorb strikes, a blunt rounded tip, rounded edges, and a hilt and pommel assembled to stay tight.
- **Source them from a reputable stage-combat armoury**, by purchase or rental. Fight directors usually know which suppliers they trust.
- **Inspect before every rehearsal and performance**: tight pommel and guard, no cracks, no bends, and no nicks or burrs on the edge (file smooth any burrs raised by blade contact). Remove anything doubtful from use.
- **Maintain**: wipe and lightly oil steel blades to stop rust, and keep grips clean and secure.
- **Professional bodies** train and certify fight directors and performers, for example the Society of American Fight Directors in the US and the British Academy of Dramatic Combat in the UK. **[Check current]**

## Daggers and knives [General knowledge]

- **Rubber or foam daggers** for any stabbing action, close fights or falls.
- **Retractable knives** have a blade that slides into the handle on contact. They must be checked before every use: a stuck blade is dangerous.
- **Kitchen knives used for real chopping** onstage (a cook preparing food) are real tools: keep the action away from other actors, store them locked, and treat them as consumable-food equipment for hygiene ([ch. 47](../7-effects/47-eating-drinking-and-smoking.md)).
- **Blood knives** release stage blood through the blade or handle ([ch. 50](../7-effects/50-blood-and-messy-effects.md)).

## Scabbards and sheaths [General knowledge]

- Make the scabbard fit the actual blade, so the blade can't slide out and can be drawn smoothly in one motion.
- Line the throat (opening) so metal doesn't scrape and the blade doesn't stick.
- Coordinate belts, frogs and hangers with the costume shop: swords are costume props as well as weapons (M 9; H 3).
- KF builds hollow foam scabbards around foam blades ([ch. 33](../5-materials/33-eva-foam.md); [build walkthrough](../5-materials/33-eva-foam-projects.md#5-takis-short-swords-and-scabbards-kf-4647)).

## Building non-combat blades

- **EVA foam** swords over a rod or PVC core, laminated, bevelled, sealed and painted (KF 10–23; [ch. 33](../5-materials/33-eva-foam.md); [sword walkthrough](../5-materials/33-eva-foam-projects.md)). KF's convention notes apply too: no functional blades, and some events ban hard materials (KF 5).
- **Wood**: carved and painted, for carried or brandished swords ([ch. 29](../5-materials/29-wood.md)).
- **Resin or rubber casts** of a real sword, for multiples ([ch. 38](../5-materials/38-molding-and-casting.md)).
- **3D-printed hilts and guards** on a safe blade ([ch. 40](../5-materials/40-3d-printing.md)).
- **Metal**: aluminium or steel blades for brandishing, dulled and balanced ([ch. 30](../5-materials/30-metal.md)).

**[Added best practice]** A foam skin doesn't make a rigid core safe for contact. Embed and pad the core's ends, and keep built swords out of fights unless the fight director approves them.

## For the classroom

- **Wall hanger vs. combat sword.** If you can, compare a decorative sword and a combat sword (or photos and specifications). Students identify the tang, blade, tip and assembly differences and explain the danger.
- **Inspection checklist.** Students write a pre-show inspection checklist for swords and daggers and use it on the stock weapons.
- **Foam sword build.** Students build a non-contact foam sword with a scabbard that draws cleanly every time (KF 10–23).
- **Use matrix.** For five scripted moments, students choose the right kind of blade from the table above and justify it.
