# 45. Other Weapons and Armor

> **Sources:** H ch.8 (p. 159), ch.10 (pp. 190–191), ch.15 (pp. 291–306); KF (pp. 5, 32–36, 38–40, 42–44); J (arms and armour entries). Most of this chapter is **[General knowledge]**.
> **[Check current]** Laws on bows, crossbows, martial-arts weapons and replica explosives vary by place. Follow your institution's weapons policy ([ch. 42](42-weapons-safety-and-policy.md)).

Beyond guns and blades, productions call for staffs, spears, axes, hammers, bows, shields, armour and more. The same principle applies to all of them: **choose the least dangerous version that tells the story**, and let the fight director decide anything used in combat ([ch. 42](42-weapons-safety-and-policy.md#choosing-the-least-dangerous-option-general-knowledge)).

## Staffs, spears and polearms [General knowledge]

- **Shafts**: hardwood or **rattan** (which flexes and splinters less than many woods) for combat staffs; aluminium or PVC for light carried spears. Check every shaft for cracks and splinters before use.
- **Heads**: foam, rubber or wood for anything that could touch a person. Rigid metal heads only for carried or static weapons.
- **Ends**: round and pad the butt end; it's what usually hits people by accident in a crowded scene.
- **Length**: long weapons are dangerous in wings, doorways and crowds. Rehearse entrances and exits with them early.
- KF builds a foam spear around a heat-bent, split PVC core (KF 42–44; [walkthrough](../5-materials/33-eva-foam-projects.md#4-aloys-spear-kf-4244)).

## Axes, hammers, clubs and maces [General knowledge]

- For contact or near-contact, use **foam or rubber heads** on a flexible or padded shaft. Film stunt weapons are often single castings of foam rubber (H 190–191).
- A head that really chops (Hart's nightly melon-splitting axe) needs a real tool-steel edge and a written safety plan (H 159).
- Weight is part of the acting. A light foam axe can be acted as heavy; a heavy real one is tiring and harder to control.

## Bows, crossbows and arrows [General knowledge]

- **Never fire a real arrow or bolt at or near anyone.** No projectile should leave a bow onstage.
- **Use unstrung or very light bows**, or bows with the string held loosely, for drawing and aiming. A drawn bow stores a lot of energy; a snapping limb or string can injure.
- **Arrows**: blunt or foam tips for carried quivers. "Arrows in the body" are separate trick props fixed to the costume or actor, revealed by staging.
- **Firing effects** are created with staging, sound and timing, not projectiles.
- Many places regulate crossbows and some bows like firearms; check the law.

## Shields [General knowledge]

- **Materials**: plywood, aluminium, plastic, laminated foam (KF's shield, KF 32–36; [walkthrough](../5-materials/33-eva-foam-projects.md#2-hylian-shield-kf-3236)), or vacuum-formed or thermoplastic shells ([ch. 31](../5-materials/31-plastics.md), [ch. 36](../5-materials/36-shells-and-laminates.md)).
- **Edges**: pad or roll them. A shield edge is a hard, wide striking surface.
- **Straps and grips** must fit the actor's arm and be checked for wear; a loose shield is uncontrollable.
- Combat shields must survive repeated blows: test with the fight director's choreography.

## Armour [General knowledge]

Armour sits between props and costumes. Agree early who builds it ([ch. 1](../1-intro/01-what-is-a-prop.md); M 9).
- **Materials**: EVA foam ([ch. 33](../5-materials/33-eva-foam.md)), thermoplastic sheets such as Worbla or Wonderflex ([ch. 36](../5-materials/36-shells-and-laminates.md)), moulded leather ([ch. 35](../5-materials/35-leather.md)), vacuum-formed plastic ([ch. 31](../5-materials/31-plastics.md)), fiberglass ([ch. 36](../5-materials/36-shells-and-laminates.md)), or metal for real armour ([ch. 30](../5-materials/30-metal.md)).
- **Movement**: build armour around how the actor moves, sits, falls and breathes. Test every piece in a fitting.
- **Heat and sweat**: armour is hot. Ventilate, line with breathable fabric, and keep paint and primers away from skin (KF leaves grips unprimed, KF 40).
- **Noise**: real metal is loud. Line plates with felt or use non-metal materials if the sound is unwanted.
- **Quick changes**: plan fastenings (buckles, magnets, Velcro, hooks) for the time available.
- KF distinguishes armour, shaped on one person's body, from props that anyone can hold (KF 2).

## Explosives, grenades and other modern weapons [General knowledge]

- **Dummy explosives** (grenades, dynamite, bombs) are non-functional props. They must look inert to anyone who sees them in transit: case them, label them, and never leave them in public view.
- **Real flash, bang and smoke effects** are pyrotechnics, which need a licensed pyrotechnician and permits ([ch. 48](../7-effects/48-flame-smoke-and-sound.md); H 7).
- **Tasers, batons, handcuffs and police equipment**: use non-functional versions; check locks and keys on real handcuffs (or use trick ones that open without a key).
- **Whips**: a whip "crack" is dangerous and needs training. Use a sound effect, or a soft whip with choreography.

## For the classroom

- **Least dangerous version.** For a battle scene with spears, a bow, a shield and an axe, students choose materials and designs for each and justify them for safety and look.
- **Armour fitting.** Students build one piece of EVA or thermoplastic armour and test it in a movement session: sit, kneel, fall, raise arms, then revise the design.
- **Shield build.** Teams build a combat-ready shield with padded edges and test it against choreographed strikes under supervision.
