# 47. Eating, Drinking and Smoking Onstage

> **Sources:** M ch.1 (pp. 7–8), ch.9 (p. 129), ch.10 (pp. 149–150), ch.11 (pp. 156–157); H ch.1 (p. 6), ch.9 (p. 181), ch.13 (pp. 250–251); FF (pp. 3–5, 194–200). Sections marked **[General knowledge]** aren't from the guide's sources.
> **[Check current]** Food-safety rules, allergen law, and smoking and vaping law vary by place and change. Your local health rules and venue policy govern.

When an actor eats, drinks or smokes, the prop goes inside someone's body, every performance. Food safety and health come first. Building *inedible* food is covered in [ch. 41](../5-materials/41-prop-food.md).

## Food and drink (M 7–8, 156–157; H 6)

- **Consumable food must be fresh for every performance.** Food poisoning has serious consequences for actors and for your reputation. Props supplies the ingredients; the run crew usually prepares the food, so **demonstrate the preparation** to them (M 8, 129).
- Ask about allergies and dietary needs **[added best practice]**.
- **Stage drinks** (M 156–157):
  - **Champagne**: ginger ale (it bubbles, and a shaken bottle still pops its cork).
  - **Dancing waiters with "full" glasses**: a disc of amber lighting gel inside the glass. This works for wine too.
  - **Dark liquor**: diluted tea, matched in a clear glass against the real thing. **Clear liquor**: water. **Wine**: juice or food coloring.
  - **Beer**: relabeled bottles of warm ginger ale or lemon-lime soda, re-capped with a bottle capper. Make printed labels for soda cans.
- **Fake food** (non-edible) (M 157):
  - Materials: clay, polystyrene, **spray foam** (expands about 3×; good for muffins and bread), casting resin, sponges, upholstery foam, chamois, Model Magic.
  - **Baker's clay** for baked goods (H 250–251).
  - Polyurethane foam cubes as an hors d'oeuvre base (H 181).
  - Real food dries and browns quickly. **Don't shellac real food** to "preserve" it. Mussman's (possibly apocryphal) story of a shellacked roast hen exploding under stage lights makes the point.
- Hart: fake food is often built because buying and preparing real food every night is expensive and wasteful (H 6).
- **For building inedible food in depth** (salt dough, candle wax, carved foam, cast food, clear-rubber drinks), see [ch. 41](../5-materials/41-prop-food.md) and [ch. 41 recipe cards](../5-materials/41-prop-food-recipes.md) (FF). For one edible bite inside a fake dish, see the [edible inset](../5-materials/41-prop-food.md#fake-and-real-together-the-edible-inset-ff-194200) (FF 194–200). Harrison & Honesty's sequel on edible props and allergens (*Volume 2*, 2025) isn't incorporated yet.

## Smoking (M 149–150)

> **[Check current]** Smoking laws, including those covering herbal cigarettes and e-cigarettes, vary widely and have changed since 2008. Check state and local law. If the law prohibits it, inform your supervisor and the artistic director; it's their call whether to accept the risk.

Where smoking is allowed:
- Use the mildest available product.
- Use **heavy ashtrays**, removably secured with putty or rolled gaff tape, with **clear liquid soap** in the bottom to snuff butts.
- Put **sand buckets** on both sides backstage for actors exiting with lit cigarettes.
- **Warn the audience.** The house manager posts signs and the stage manager supplies the details. Refund policy is the box office's call.

## Food safety for edible props [General knowledge]

- **Choose food the actor can eat easily, quickly and repeatedly**: soft, quiet to chew, quick to swallow, not crumbly or dry (crumbs make actors cough), and not messy on costumes. Bread, bananas, soft cake, gelatin and peeled fruit are common choices.
- **Allergies and diets**: ask every actor who eats or drinks, and anyone who handles the food, about allergies, intolerances, religious and ethical diets. Keep a written record and label containers.
- **Hygiene**: wash hands, use gloves and clean utensils, keep a clean food-prep area away from paint and glue, and keep cold food cold.
- **Fresh every performance** (M 8). Throw away anything left over; never reuse food that has been onstage.
- **Clean props that touch mouths** (cups, cutlery, plates, pipes) after every use, and don't share between actors.
- **The edible inset**: for one bite inside a fake dish, set a small piece of real food behind a food-safe barrier (FF 194–200; [ch. 41](../5-materials/41-prop-food.md#fake-and-real-together-the-edible-inset-ff-194200)).

## Smoking alternatives [General knowledge]

- **Don't light anything** if you can avoid it. Unlit cigarettes, cigars and pipes are often enough.
- **Herbal cigarettes** still produce smoke and are covered by many smoking bans.
- **Prop cigarettes** with a powder that puffs out like smoke, and battery cigarettes with a glowing LED tip, avoid flame entirely.
- **E-cigarettes and vapes** may be banned in the venue or by law; check.
- Any lit smoking material is live flame: see [ch. 48](48-flame-smoke-and-sound.md).

## For the classroom

- **Stage-drinks lab.** Students produce convincing champagne, whiskey, red wine and beer for a scene, and present their solutions for nightly prep and cleanup (M 156–157).
- **Eating scene plan.** For a scripted meal, students choose edible foods for each actor, write an allergy check form, and a nightly prep and cleanup procedure.
- **Smoke without fire.** Students compare three non-flame smoking options for look, sound and audience impact.
