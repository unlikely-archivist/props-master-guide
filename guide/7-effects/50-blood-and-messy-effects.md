# 50. Blood and Messy Effects

> **Sources:** M ch.1 (p. 8); H ch.1 (p. 3), ch.14 (pp. 274–275). Most of this chapter is **[General knowledge]**.
> **[Check current]** Commercial stage-blood products change; check each one's ingredients for allergens, staining and washability.

## Blood and messy effects (M 8; H 3)

Stage blood is a consumable. Plan cleaning, costume coordination (wardrobe needs to know the blood and the fabric) and replacement quantities. *[Pending: Effects Guidebook ch. 7, "Liquid Delivery."]*

## Choosing stage blood [General knowledge]

- **Washable vs. permanent.** Washable bloods come out of costumes but may look thinner; permanent or "film" bloods look rich but stain. Test on the actual costume fabric with wardrobe before you choose.
- **Mouth-safe blood** is made for use in the mouth (usually syrup-based). Only use bloods sold as mouth-safe in mouths, and check ingredients for allergens.
- **Fresh vs. dried.** Fresh blood is bright and runny; dried or old blood is darker and brown. Scab and gel bloods hold shape for wounds.
- **Lighting changes colour.** Test the blood under show lighting; some reds turn brown or pink under certain gels.

## Delivery [General knowledge]

- **Blood packs and capsules**: small bags or capsules burst by the actor's hand or teeth.
- **Blood knives and razors**: channels in the blade or handle release blood as it's drawn.
- **Tubing rigs**: a hidden tube under the costume feeds blood from a squeeze bulb or syringe. Coordinate with costumes for routing and cleanup.
- **Pre-bloodied props and costume pieces**: swapped in during a blackout or offstage.
- **Gelatin breakaway prosthetics** can hold blood for wound effects (H 274–275; [ch. 46](46-effects-and-breakaways.md#breakaways-h-34-h-77-190191-274275)).

## Cleanup and safety [General knowledge]

- **Floors**: blood on stage is a slip hazard. Plan when and how it's wiped up, and use non-slip treatments where actors walk.
- **Costumes and props**: plan washing, replacement and drying time with wardrobe for every performance.
- **Hygiene**: each actor uses their own blood capsules and mouth props; never share.
- **Other messes** (food fights, water, mud, powder, confetti) need the same planning: what it does to the floor, costumes, set and audience, and who cleans it up.

## For the classroom

- **Blood test.** Students test three stage bloods on costume fabric swatches, wash them, and photograph the results under show lighting.
- **Effect plan.** For a stabbing scene, students design the blood delivery, the reset, the cleanup schedule and the costume plan with wardrobe.
