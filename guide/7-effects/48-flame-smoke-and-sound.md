# 48. Flame, Smoke and Live Sound

> **Sources:** M ch.2 (p. 34), ch.1 (pp. 9–10), ch.10 (pp. 150–151); H ch.1 (p. 7), ch.16 (p. 320). Sections marked **[General knowledge]** aren't from the guide's sources.
> **[Check current]** Fire codes, flame permits, pyrotechnic licensing and rules on haze and fog are set by local authorities and venues, and change. Your fire marshal has the final word.

## Live flame (M 150–151; M 10)

- **Have working, inspected fire extinguishers onstage before any live flame**, even a match.
- Mussman: avoid live flame whenever possible. A battery candle takes a few dollars and a few hours; one real candle takes weeks of planning.
- If flame is unavoidable:
  - Use **wide, freestanding pillar candles** or securely held candles.
  - Light Sterno-type torches just before the entrance and extinguish them immediately on exit.
  - Flame-proof the surrounding fabrics and finishes.
- Fire codes and the fire marshal govern live flame. A permit may be required **[check current]**.

## Live sound effects (M 34, 9)

Some directors and sound designers prefer live sound. Props may supply:
- **crash boxes** (a wooden box of metal and plastic junk to shake or drop)
- **thunder sheets**
- **door-slam effects**
- offstage gunshot effects (see [ch. 43](../6-weapons/43-firearms.md))

## Flame-effect alternatives [General knowledge]

- **LED candles, lanterns and torches**: cheap, safe, and now very convincing, including flickering models. Electrics can sometimes control them from the board ([ch. 49](49-practicals-and-mechanisms.md)).
- **Silk-flame effects**: fabric "flames" blown upward by a fan and lit from below, for fires and torches.
- **Projection and lighting** for fireplaces and burning buildings.

## Smoke, haze and fog [General knowledge]

- **Haze and fog machines** are usually the lighting or effects department's equipment, but props may supply smoking objects (a smouldering pot, a steaming cup).
- **Small smoke effects** in props can use small fog units, ultrasonic misters (for "steam"), or commercial smoke powders. Check that any fluid or powder is approved for theatrical use and for your venue.
- **Smoke can trigger fire alarms.** Coordinate with the venue so detectors are managed correctly; never disable alarms yourself.
- **Health**: some performers are sensitive to haze and fog. Productions often notify the company and audience in advance.

## Pyrotechnics [General knowledge]

Anything that flashes, bangs, burns fiercely or explodes is a pyrotechnic. It needs a **licensed pyrotechnician** and usually a permit (H 7). Props departments should not buy, store or fire pyrotechnics.

## Live sound effects in practice [General knowledge]

- **Crash boxes** need a safe drop zone and a consistent drop; tape the lid or contents so pieces can't escape.
- **Door slams** can use a slam box (a small door in a frame) offstage, timed to the action.
- **Gunshots** without a gun are covered in [ch. 43](../6-weapons/43-firearms.md#alternatives-to-firing-a-gun-onstage-general-knowledge).
- Agree with the sound designer and stage manager who operates each effect and on what cue.

## For the classroom

- **Flame alternatives.** Students build or buy three versions of a candle-lit scene (real candle, LED candle, LED candle with a diffuser and flicker) and compare them from the house.
- **Fire plan.** For a scene with a lit oil lamp, students write a flame safety plan: extinguisher placement, who lights and extinguishes it, flame-proofing, and the permit process (M 150–151).
- **Sound effects kit.** Teams build a crash box, a thunder sheet and a slam box and perform a short sequence on cue.
