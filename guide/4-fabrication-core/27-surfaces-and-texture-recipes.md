Recipe cards and step-by-step tutorials for [ch. 27](27-surfaces-and-texture.md). Read the chapter for the principles and safety notes. Sourced cards carry page cites; steps marked **[General knowledge]** aren't from the guide's sources.

## Tutorial: a "painted many times" door panel (H 311–312)

1. Prime a scrap of plywood or MDF.
2. Trowel on a thin coat of scenic dope.
3. While it's wet, **drag a loop of hemp rope** through it ("roping") for the look of many paint layers, or **comb** it for weathered wood.
4. Let it dry fully (it can take days for thick coats).
5. Base-coat, then glaze and drybrush to bring out the texture ([ch. 28 recipes](28-painting-and-finishing-recipes.md)).

## Tutorial: plaster-stencilled ornament (H 312)

1. Cut a stencil from thick card or plastic sheet.
2. Hold or tape it on the surface and **trowel joint compound or dope through it**.
3. Screed it flat with a straightedge across the stencil.
4. Lift the stencil straight up. For thicker relief, let it dry and build another layer.

## Tutorial: distressing a wooden box (H 317–319; M 158–159)

1. **Write the object's life story** first: age, use, storage, repairs, accidents (H 318–319).
2. Build the box well and finish it as new.
3. **Wear the handling areas**: sand, rasp or file the edges, corners and wherever hands go.
4. **Beat it** with a chain or a bag of nuts and bolts for dents (H 318).
5. Optional: torch-scorch and wire-brush to raise the grain (keep an extinguisher handy).
6. Darken the recesses and dents with a wash; lighten the high points with a drybrush ([ch. 28](28-painting-and-finishing.md#breaking-down-h-340342)).
7. Check it against the story: is the wear where the object was actually handled?

## Tutorial: field flame test [General knowledge]

Your fire marshal's procedure takes priority (H 320).
1. Cut a sample of the finished, treated material (fabric, paper, foam, finished prop surface).
2. Work outdoors or over a non-flammable surface, with water and an extinguisher ready.
3. Hold a match flame to the bottom edge for the time your local procedure states.
4. Remove the flame. Note how long it keeps burning, whether it flares, and whether it drips.
5. It passes if it self-extinguishes within the set time without flaring or dripping (H 320). Record the result with a photo and date.

## Quick reference: which coating for foam? (H 315–317)

See the full table in [ch. 27](27-surfaces-and-texture.md#coating-foam-h-315317). In brief: flexible coats for soft foam, hard sandable shells for rigid foam, laminates for strength. **Polyester resin dissolves polystyrene**; epoxy and polyurethane don't (H 317).
