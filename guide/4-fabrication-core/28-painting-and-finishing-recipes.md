# Painting and Finishing Recipes (recipes and tutorials for ch. 28)

Paint "recipes" and step-by-step finishes for [ch. 28](28-painting-and-finishing.md). Hart's advice applies to all of them: think in recipes built from technique "ingredients," and **keep treatments simple enough to reproduce** (H 333). Steps marked **[General knowledge]** aren't from the guide's sources.

> **[Check current]** Product names (Design Master Glossy Wood Tone, Preval, Rit, Procion, cold gun blue) and formulations change. Read the SDS for shellac, solvent dyes, stains and patinas.

| Recipe | Mix | Use | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shadow wash** | Paint thinned with half water, half alcohol, plus a drop of dish soap | Flows into recesses; wipe the high points | H 337 |

| Look | Base | Then |

| **Leather** | Black | Brown drybrush |
| **Wood grain** | Base colour | Thinner grain colour, dragged while wet with a rocker grainer or graining comb; tone down with a glaze |
| **Marble** | Wet-on-wet base | Glaze, spatter, feather-dragged veins, sponging, glaze |
| **Luminous depth** | Any | Many translucent glazes with mica, bronze or pearlescent powders |

## Tutorial: faux wood grain on MDF (H 334–340)

1. Seal the MDF edges with 50/50 PVA and water; prime (H 334).
2. Base-coat in the lightest wood colour, **brushing in the direction the grain will run** (H 335).
3. Mix a darker, thinner grain colour (a glaze).
4. Brush the glaze over one section at a time.
5. While wet, drag a **rubber rocker grainer** through it, rocking it for knots, or comb it, shifting the comb for variation (H 339).
6. Soften with a dry brush if needed; let dry.
7. Start high-contrast, then unify with an overall brown glaze (H 336, 339).
8. Seal with a satin clear coat (H 319; [ch. 27](27-surfaces-and-texture.md#protective-coats-h-319320)).

## Tutorial: aged brass on a plastic or foam prop (H 333–339)

1. Prime with a primer that suits the material ([ch. 28](28-painting-and-finishing.md#priming-h-333334)).
2. Base-coat dark brown (H 339).
3. Drybrush gold, then a lighter gold on the highest edges.
4. Shadow-wash greenish-brown into the recesses for patina (H 337, 341).
5. Seal with a satin coat so the metallic doesn't rub off on hands (H 319).

## Tutorial: rust on metal or a metal-look prop [General knowledge]

1. Base-coat dark brown or black.
2. Sponge or stipple burnt sienna and orange in patches, concentrated on **edges, corners and where water would sit** (H 341).
3. Spatter a little yellow ochre and dark brown.
4. For texture, mix a little fine sand or sawdust into the paint for the heaviest rust (H 315).
5. Drybrush a little steel grey on raised edges if bare metal would show through.

## Tutorial: metal leaf (H 349–351)

1. Seal the surface (acrylic gesso if you want to sand it smooth).
2. Paint the **bole**: red to warm gold, black for a cold Art Deco look.
3. Brush on **gold size** and wait until it's tacky.
4. Lay the leaf with a static-charged brush. It can't be repositioned.
5. Brush off the excess and burnish; patch missed spots.
6. Seal with shellac (amber shellac warms it) or polyurethane or acrylic.

## Tutorial: dyeing a fabric swatch (H 345–347)

1. Identify the fibre, and choose the matching dye class ([ch. 28](28-painting-and-finishing.md#dyes-h-345348)).
2. Use stainless steel pots and dedicated tools.
3. **Paste powder dye with a little water first**; dissolve salts separately. **Add acid to water, never water to acid.**
4. Heat to the right temperature (around 190°F for silk and wool; cotton and linen can boil).
5. Wet the fabric, add it, and stir often. **Test swatches first**, because wet fabric looks darker.
6. Rinse until the water runs clear, and use a fixative if the dye calls for one.

## Tutorial: staining wood (H 348–349)

1. Test on a scrap of the same wood.
2. Brush or rag the stain on.
3. Wipe off immediately, or after 10–30 minutes, or after hours, depending on the depth wanted.
4. Layer stains for complex colour. End grain soaks up more.
5. Let it dry **away from actors and coworkers**, especially oil-based stain.
6. **Seal it**, or it rubs off on skin and costumes.
