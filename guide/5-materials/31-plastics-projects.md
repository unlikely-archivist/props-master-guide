# Plastics Projects (projects for ch. 31)

Starter projects and quick references for [ch. 31](31-plastics.md). Heated plastic fumes are a mix no respirator cartridge is rated for: **use the minimum heat and extract the fumes** (H 172). Steps marked **[General knowledge]** aren't from the guide's sources.

## Project 1: identify the plastic (H 170–171)

1. Collect unknown plastic scraps.
2. Check recycling codes where present.
3. **Acetone test**: dab acetone on a hidden spot. If it turns tacky, the plastic can be solvent-welded (acrylic, polycarbonate, styrene, ABS, PVC). If not, it may be polyethylene, polypropylene, nylon or acetal, which won't solvent-weld or take most glues and paints (H 170–171).
4. Record the results for the shop's reference shelf.

## Project 2: a set of heat-bent acrylic brackets (H 172–174)

**Teaches:** strip-heater bending and jigs.
1. Keep the protective film on and mark the bend lines on it (H 175–176).
2. Build a wooden jig at the bend angle you need.
3. Heat along the line on a **strip heater**, heating the *outside* of the bend (both sides for sheet over ¼") (H 172–173). Acrylic forms at about 240–340°F (H 172).
4. Bend over the jig and hold until cool, because the plastic springs back (H 173–174).
5. Make five identical brackets. Compare their angles.

## Project 3: a vacuum-formed panel (H 174–175)

**Teaches:** bucks, draft and detail loss.
1. Build a buck from MDF, rigid foam (coated) or plaster, with **no undercuts** and slightly sloped sides (H 174–175).
2. Exaggerate fine detail, because it softens through the sheet.
3. Raise the buck on a spacer so the sheet can wrap the edges without webbing (H 178).
4. Heat 0.06" HIPS until it sags evenly (H 174–175).
5. Lower it over the buck and run the vacuum until the plastic has cooled.
6. Dust the buck with talc and pop the part off with compressed air.
7. Trim, and paint with a primer that bites into plastic ([ch. 28](../4-fabrication-core/28-painting-and-finishing.md#priming-h-333334)).

## Project 4: a solvent-welded box (H 170–171)

1. Cut acrylic or styrene panels with clean, square (unpolished) edges.
2. Tape the box together dry.
3. Run **water-thin cement** along each joint from a needle bottle and let capillary action draw it in.
4. Leave it clamped snug, not crushed, and don't handle it for 24 hours.
5. Wear the right gloves and respirator: solvents absorb through skin and harm with the first breath.

## Project 5: polishing a clear edge (H 175–179)

1. Scrape the saw marks off with a razor held perpendicular to the edge.
2. **Wet-sand** through grits up to about 600.
3. Buff with muslin and flannel wheels and fine compound.
4. Clean with soap and water, **never ammonia**.

## Quick reference

**Forming temperatures (°F)** (H 172): acrylic 240–340 · HIPS 325–350 · ABS 300–350 · Kydex 350–365 · polycarbonate 350–425 · Sintra ~300 · PETG 230–350 · CAB 335–390 · polyethylene 325–425.

**Vacuum-forming sheet** (H 174–175): HIPS, 0.06" for general use, about 0.08" for torso armour; thicker for tall bucks.

**Weldable vs. not** (H 170–171): acrylic, polycarbonate, styrene, ABS and PVC weld; polyethylene, polypropylene, Teflon, acetal and nylon don't.
