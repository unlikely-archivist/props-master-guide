# Leather Projects (projects for ch. 35)

Starter projects for [ch. 35](35-leather.md). Steps marked **[General knowledge]** aren't from the guide's sources. Ventilate and wear gloves when using spirit dyes and solvent cements.

## Project 1: a saddle-stitched belt pouch (H 218–220)

**Teaches:** patterning, cutting, gluing, punching, saddle stitching, edge finishing.
1. Pattern the pouch in card: front, back with flap, and gusset ([ch. 24](../4-fabrication-core/24-patterning.md)).
2. Cut from 4–6 oz vegetable-tanned leather with a knife and straightedge (H 218–220).
3. **Skive** the edges thin from the back where layers overlap (H 218–220).
4. Mark stitch spacing with a stitch marker; on heavy leather cut a stitching groove (H 218–220).
5. Glue the pieces together with barge or contact cement, then punch the holes with a **diamond awl** (H 218–220).
6. **Saddle stitch** with waxed thread and two needles, passing through every hole from opposite sides. Allow up to 7× the seam length in thread; backstitch two holes to finish (H 218–220).
7. **[General knowledge]** Bevel and burnish the edges, dye, and seal with a flexible finish ([ch. 35](35-leather.md#tooling-dyeing-and-finishing-general-knowledge)).
8. Fit a belt loop, snap or buckle with the matching setter.

## Project 2: a wet-moulded bracer or mask (H 293)

**Teaches:** moulding leather over a form.
1. Carve or build a positive form (wood, foam with a hard coat, or a plaster cast).
2. Soak vegetable-tanned leather (15 minutes to 12 hours).
3. Drape it over the form and pin it in the **trim areas** only (steel pins stain).
4. Work it down with a **sticketta** for 20–60 minutes until it holds; leave slack for the valleys.
5. Hammer in details as it dries, then burnish.
6. For a harder piece, dip it briefly (2 minutes or less) in boiling water, or bake it low (120–175°F) for **cuir bouilli** (H 293).
7. Trim, dye and seal.

## Project 3: a tooled panel [General knowledge]

1. **Case** the leather: dampen it evenly and wait until the surface just starts to return to its natural colour.
2. Transfer a design with a stylus through tracing film.
3. Cut the lines with a swivel knife.
4. Bevel and background with stamps and a mallet.
5. Dye, antique (to fill the tooled lines) and finish.

## Quick reference

**Thickness** (H 218–220): 1 oz ≈ 1/64". Tooling leather runs 3–10 oz; 8–9 oz is the favourite.

**Thread** (H 218–220): waxed sinew or linen for hand-stitched tooling leather; polyester or nylon for machine-sewn garment leather (cotton rots against leather).

**Only vegetable-tanned leather** can be carved, stamped, embossed or moulded (H 218–220).
