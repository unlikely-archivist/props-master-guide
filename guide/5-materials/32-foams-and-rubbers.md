# 32. Foams and Rubbers

> **Sources:** H ch.9 (pp. 179–181), ch.12 (p. 236), ch.13 (pp. 243, 252–254), ch.16 (pp. 315–317); M ch.10 (pp. 143–144); FF (pp. 13, 26–29, 119–128, 145–157); KF (whole book). Sections marked **[General knowledge]** aren't from the guide's sources.
> **[Check current]** Foam product names, densities and formulations change, and some foams can't be recycled. Check the SDS for every foam, glue and coating.

Foam is the prop maker's most-used bulk material: light, cheap, carvable and quick. This chapter is the hub for all foams. It covers the families and points to the chapters that go deeper: [EVA foam](33-eva-foam.md), [carving rigid foam](37-sculpting.md#materials), [coating foam](../4-fabrication-core/27-surfaces-and-texture.md#coating-foam-h-315317), [foam in food props](41-prop-food.md#rigid-foam-xps-ff-13-and-chs-4-17-22-23-27-30-39-41) and [expanding foam](41-prop-food.md#expanding-urethane-foam-flexfoam-it-ff-18-2631-7189-165167-171173-184186-218222).

## Rubbers and foams (H 179–181; H 236)

- **Elastomer** is the technical word for rubbery polymers.
- **Open-cell foam** is sponge-like and absorbent. **Closed-cell foam** is rigid, stable and waterproof.

| Material | Uses |
|---|---|
| **Latex** | Liquid for casting, coating and makeup effects. Vulcanized into rubber. **Allergy risk** |
| **Neoprene, nitrile** | Casting (neoprene); chemical gloves |
| **EPDM** (pond liner, roofing rubber) | Durable elastic parts, padding |
| **Polyethylene foam**: Ethafoam backer rod, pool noodles, yoga mats | **Halved backer rod makes curved molding.** Thermoformable |
| **EVA foam** ("craft foam", floor mats) | **Lightweight armor, masks, character heads.** Heat-formable. **Hot glue bonds it** (the same polymer). Pattern it like paper, beveling convex joints ([ch. 24](../4-fabrication-core/24-patterning.md)). KF uses contact cement as its main EVA adhesive instead (KF 15; [ch. 33](33-eva-foam.md#where-the-sources-disagree)) |
| **Soft polyurethane foam** ("polyfoam") | Upholstery, puppets |
| **Rigid polyurethane foam** | Fine-detail carving. More toxic. Taxidermy suppliers stock it |

**Cutting foam**: band saw for dense foam; a **knife-edged blade** for soft foam (coarse teeth can grab and drag soft foam into the blade); a foam cutter; or a fully extended snap knife with a drop of sewing-machine oil on the blade (H 181).

For food props made from XPS, upholstery foam, backer rod and expanding urethane foam (FlexFoam-iT!), see [ch. 41](41-prop-food.md) (FF).

For building whole props from EVA foam (densities and thicknesses, PVC and rod cores, carving, textures, foam clay, heat sealing and flexible primers), see [ch. 33](33-eva-foam.md) (KF).

## Rigid foams for carving (H 243, 252–254; FF 13, 26–29, 145–157)

| Foam | Character | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| **XPS** (extruded polystyrene; pink or blue insulation board) | Fine, even cells; carves cleanly; glue seams vanish | Dissolved by most solvents and spray paints. Fragile, so **coat it**. Glue with water-based contact cement ("green glue"), wood glue or PVA; avoid solvent sprays (H 252–253; FF 26–29, 157) |
| **EPS** (expanded polystyrene; bead foam) | Coarser beads; comes in huge blocks; packaging foam is less dense | Good for big sculptures; beads tear out when carved (H 253) |
| **Floral and craft foam** | Brittle; often sold as "Styrofoam" | Not the same as XPS; crumbles (H 253) |
| **Rigid urethane foam** | Denser; holds finer detail | Taxidermy suppliers; or cast your own from A-B foam (H 253–254) |
| **Spray foam** | Fills irregular shapes over armatures | Cures from the outside, so apply in thin layers. **Isocyanates**: use local exhaust (H 254) |

Full carving methods are in [ch. 37](37-sculpting.md). Laminating tables for XPS are in [ch. 41](41-prop-food.md#rigid-foam-xps-ff-13-and-chs-4-17-22-23-27-30-39-41).

**Safety** (H 243; M 143–144): hot-wiring or hot-knifing polystyrene releases styrene vapour; rasping makes dust. Ventilate (local exhaust for big hot-wire rigs), use an organic-vapour respirator per the SDS for hot cutting, and a particulate mask and goggles for rasping ([ch. 37](37-sculpting.md#polystyrene-safety-h-243-m-143144)).

## Coating foam (H 315–317)

Bare foam dents, tears and dissolves under many paints. Choose a coating by what the prop must survive: flexible coats for soft foam, hard sandable shells for rigid foam, laminates for strength. **Polyester resin dissolves polystyrene**; epoxy and polyurethane don't (H 317). The full table is in [ch. 27](../4-fabrication-core/27-surfaces-and-texture.md#coating-foam-h-315317). For EVA foam, see heat sealing and flexible primers in [ch. 33](33-eva-foam.md#filling-and-priming-kf-2829-36-40-47).

## Working with foam [General knowledge]

- **Density matters.** Foams are sold by density (weight per volume). Higher density is stronger, heavier and holds detail better; lower density is lighter and cheaper but crushes and tears more.
- **Hot-wire cutters** cut polystyrene cleanly and fast, using a heated nichrome wire. Use the lowest heat that cuts, keep the wire moving, ventilate, and never use one on polyurethane or PVC foams, which give off much more hazardous fumes.
- **Upholstery (polyurethane) foam** is cut with an electric carving knife or a long sharp blade, glued with foam spray adhesive, and graded by density and firmness. Wrap it in batting for upholstery ([ch. 34](34-fabric-and-upholstery.md)).
- **Puppet and soft-sculpture foams** (reticulated foam, upholstery foam) are carved with scissors and glued with contact cement, then covered with fabric or latex.
- **Foam is flammable.** Many foams burn fast and release toxic smoke. Keep them away from heat sources, and check flame-retardancy requirements for anything going onstage ([ch. 27](../4-fabrication-core/27-surfaces-and-texture.md#flame-retardancy-h-320)).
- **Store foam flat and out of sunlight.** UV breaks down many foams, and they yellow and crumble with age.

## For the classroom

- **Foam sampler.** Students make a sampler board of six foams (XPS, EPS, EVA, upholstery, polyethylene, rigid urethane), carving, gluing and coating a sample of each, and label what each does well.
- **Glue test.** Test five adhesives on XPS and record which melt it, which hold, and which leave a carvable seam.
- **Coat comparison.** Coat four identical carved XPS rocks with different coatings, then drop-test and scratch-test them ([ch. 27](../4-fabrication-core/27-surfaces-and-texture.md#coating-foam-h-315317)).
