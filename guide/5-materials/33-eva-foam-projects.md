# EVA Foam Build Walkthroughs (projects for ch. 33)

Condensed notes on the five worked builds in **KF**: Svetlana Quindt (Kamui Cosplay), *The Book of Foam Props* (self-published, 2019). Headings carry KF's printed page ranges. The techniques, safety notes and source disagreements behind these cards are in [ch. 33](33-eva-foam.md). Read that chapter first.

> **[Check current]** Product names (Flexbond, PlastiDip, Kwikseal, Liquitex, Vallejo, Schmincke, Lukas, Frog Tape) are from 2019. KF links to free downloadable blueprints for the sword, shield and spear on its author's website, and to making-of videos on YouTube. Links and availability may have changed.

KF builds replicas of existing video-game weapons. The cards are useful as **method sequences**: the order of operations, material choices and time spent transfer to any foam prop, including one built from a production designer's drawing.

**Card format:** **Reference and scale**, **structure**, **details**, **finish**, **time** (where KF gives it), and **lessons** KF draws from the build. "HD" and "LD" = high- and low-density EVA foam. "Drum" = the rotary tool's sanding drum. "Contact" = contact cement.

### 1. Master Sword (KF 10–13, 20–23, 30)

- **Reference and scale:** A clean front view of an older version of the sword from image search, plus a side view traced from a screenshot of an online 3D model. Both were vector-traced (main parts at 2 pt, sanding lines at 1 pt) and lined up with guides (KF 10–11). Paper dummies were printed at 90–110 cm and checked in a mirror. Built at **100 cm** (KF 13, 20). Two prints: one kept whole, one cut up (KF 20).
- **Blade:** Traced on **two layers of 10 mm HD** for a 20 mm blade (KF 20). Knife-cut a V-groove at the centre of each layer, laid a long **aluminium rod** in contact cement and closed the layers over it, with the rod running past the blade for the grip (KF 21). The edge-line pattern pieces marked the bevel width. Rough-carved with a knife, finished with the drum, then smoothed with **120-grit by hand** (KF 21).
- **Grip and guard:** Two halves of a round foam dowel around the exposed rod, a tight **PVC pipe** slid over them. The round centre of the guard was stacked 10 mm layers sanded to a cone, glued where blade meets grip. Each guard wing was **two 10 mm and one 5 mm layer**, with bevels traced front and back from smaller pattern pieces, then knife-roughed and drum-finished. This was the hardest and slowest part and took several tries (KF 22).
- **Details:** Wings glued to the blade. Small details front and back. A layered, sanded pommel. **2 mm strips** wrapped on the grip. All checked against the blueprint, others' foam builds and an action-figure miniature (KF 23).
- **Finish:** Heat-sealed, with fuzz removed by tweezers and scissors (KF 23). **Three coats of Flexbond** with soft flat or make-up brushes; some brush marks remained (KF 30). Brushed acrylics: a liquid silver on the blade, heavy-body blues, purples, teals and golds elsewhere. Then a **flexible satin-gloss spray varnish** (KF 30).
- **Lessons:** The thin aluminium core wobbled at 100 cm. KF says steel or fiberglass would have been better (KF 21). The build shows "a great looking foam prop doesn't require any fancy tools" (KF 30).

### 2. Hylian Shield (KF 32–36)

- **Reference and scale:** Blueprint from online colour and shape references (KF avoided playing the game). Scaled to match the sword. Printed over **four A3 sheets** (KF 32).
- **Base:** Half the outline traced on **10 mm black LD**, with the centre edge cut on a **slight curve** instead of straight. The pattern was mirrored for the other half. Halves glued with contact cement from top to bottom. Forcing the curved edges together domed the shield (KF 32–33).
- **Holding the curve:** Heat-shaped by hand for more curve. Then KF built a **second, slightly larger** two-piece base the same way and **laminated** the two curved layers together, because a single large heat-shaped layer would flatten over time (KF 33).
- **Sheet size:** Puzzle mats are too small even for half this shield. KF uses jumbo **1 × 0.5 m** sheets, which also leave usable scraps (KF 33).
- **Details:** Border, swirls and triangles in **gray HD** (easier to carve, less drum slip). Pattern pieces mirrored and cut at 90°. Bevel lines marked, knife-roughed, drum-finished. Pyramid holes in the border made with three 45° cuts and the plug pulled out (done after sanding; KF says it should have been first). Placement marked from the remaining paper templates, then glued piece by piece (KF 34). Bird emblem in **2 mm**, cut with small curved scissors. Layered edges drum-cleaned. Strap added. Back-side details left off because nobody sees them (KF 35).
- **Finish:** Sanded details were heat-sealed before gluing, then the whole shield again (KF 35). **Kwikseal** in several layers on the centre seam. **Three thick coats of PlastiDip** in a spray booth with respirator and gloves. Then **three coats of Flexbond** on the sanded border and swirls only, since PlastiDip doesn't smooth (KF 36). Airbrushed silver with gunmetal shadows on the raised parts, masked with **Frog Tape**, then blue base and shadows. Tape pulled for a clean edge. Triangles (same gold acrylics as the sword) and bird by brush. Brighter highlight lines. Thick coat of **satin spray varnish** (KF 36).
- **Time:** About **6 h** build, **2 h** priming, **3–4 h** painting (KF 36).

### 3. Nergigante Claws (KF 38–40)

- **Reference and scale:** Blueprint from the official artbook, printed at several sizes, taped onto the arm, photographed and compared with the in-game avatar (KF 38).
- **Structure:** A **PVC pipe inside a hollow block of layered foam** forms each grip. KF calls this sturdy, comfortable base "probably the most important part" because everything else attaches to it (KF 38). Claw fingers cut several times from the pattern and glued around a **thinner PVC pipe**, then knife-carved and drum-smoothed. A stub of PVC left at the back of each claw plugs into a matching hole in the grip, fixed with **plenty of hot glue** (KF 38–39).
- **Blending:** Water brushed on, then **foam clay** smeared into the gaps between claws and grip and smoothed by finger (KF 39).
- **Spikes:** Small ones sculpted in foam clay (over 825 for the whole costume). Large ones are hollow pyramids of **four long 5 mm triangles**. Every spike and claw got carved scratches so they look grown. Large spikes glued on with contact cement, small ones filling the gaps. Foam clay around every spike base to hide the glue line (KF 39).
- **Skin:** Several **2 mm layers** over the whole prop to hide bumps, then a **scale texture burned in** with a soldering iron. KF extracted the fumes to a spray booth and wore a respirator (KF 40).
- **Finish:** Heat-sealed. **Three coats of Flexbond** on sanded areas (claws and large spikes), then **three coats of PlastiDip** over everything. **Inside of the grips left bare** to keep primer and paint off sweaty hands (KF 40). Flat airbrushed base, sealed with **spray varnish**. **Dark brown oil paint** smeared over everything and mostly wiped off with paper towels and a little turpentine, leaving the scales and recesses dark. Strong final coat of **satin-gloss varnish** (KF 40).
- **Lessons:** Large, unfamiliar shapes start rough and get rebuilt. Trial and error is "the only way to find solutions that actually work" (KF 38).

### 4. Aloy's Spear (KF 42–44)

- **Reference:** A blueprint loosely based on the developer's official **cosplay reference kit** (blurry images), plus in-game screenshots and **filmed turnarounds**. It still contained guesses, so all references stayed open on screen throughout the build (KF 42).
- **Core:** **PVC pipe, heat-bent**, then **split in two for transport**, with the joint directly under the round plates mid-staff. Wrapped in **2 mm EVA** and carved with wood grain using the drum. Every other part attaches to this staff, placed using the cut-out blueprint (KF 42).
- **Curved "armor" plates:** Heat shaping didn't hold, so they were curved and **laminated to a second curved layer** (the shield technique) (KF 43).
- **Details:** Stacked **2, 5 and 10 mm** pieces cut from ever-smaller pattern pieces, glued and drum-smoothed (KF 43). Carved lines on the "wooden" grip were drawn, then **burned in with a soldering iron**. High-density foam melts less, so there was little risk of burning through to the PVC (KF 43). **Basket-woven 2 mm strips** (KF 44). An uncut second blueprint lay on the floor as a guide, because the working copy was being cut away (KF 44).
- **Finish:** Heat-sealed, **three coats of PlastiDip**, airbrushed and weathered. Feathers, beads and a long blue cord added (KF 44).
- **Lessons:** KF had to tear off the back of one part after realising, too late, that it was round, not rectangular, because she missed one screenshot (KF 43). One more detail was found missing after priming and was left off (KF 44). "Take your time and do your homework" on reference before building (KF 44).

### 5. Taki's Short Swords and Scabbards (KF 46–47)

- **Reference:** Patterns for both swords and their scabbards, which had to fit each other (KF 46).
- **Blades:** Mostly **HD** for stiffness. **Two 5 mm layers** per blade, a sanded groove down the middle, a **thin aluminium rod** (no wobble at this length), closed with contact cement. Drum-sanded, then **120-grit by hand** to remove bumps and scratches (KF 46).
- **Scabbards:** **Three 5 mm layers plus two 2 mm layers**. The centre was cut out of the inner layers and the top and bottom closed with **5 mm** pieces, leaving room for the blade. Raw foam drags; priming, painting and sealing help, and you can widen the scabbard by heating it gently with a PVC rod inside. The finished swords fit (KF 46).
- **Details:** Pattern pieces marked where to glue. Most details cut from **2 mm** with nail scissors. Anything "too three-dimensional" was sculpted in **foam clay** (KF 47).
- **Finish:** Seams closed with **Kwikseal**. **Three thick coats of Flexbond**. Airbrushed metal finish (KF 47).
- **Time:** About a day of construction (KF 47).
- **Lessons:** KF recommends a small project like this as a first prop (KF 47).
