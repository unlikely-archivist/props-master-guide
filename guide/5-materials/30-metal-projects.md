# Metal Projects (projects for ch. 30)

Starter projects and quick references for [ch. 30](30-metal.md). Hart recommends learning welding by building a small object rather than running practice beads (see the chapter's classroom prompts). Steps marked **[General knowledge]** aren't from the guide's sources. **Complete welding and grinder safety training first** ([ch. 30](30-metal.md#welding-safety-h-142144-w-62); [ch. 20](../4-fabrication-core/20-health-and-safety.md)).

## Project 1: a folded sheet-metal tray (H 140–141)

**Teaches:** patterning, cutting with snips, folding safe edges, riveting.
1. Pattern the tray in cardstock first, including tabs for the corners ([ch. 24](../4-fabrication-core/24-patterning.md)).
2. Trace onto thin aluminium or mild steel sheet.
3. Cut with aviation snips: **yellow = straight, red = left, green = right** (H 133–134).
4. **Fold every edge over** so no raw edge is exposed (H 140–141).
5. Fold the sides on a homemade **brake** (a clamp board and hinged bar) (H 140–141).
6. Join the corner tabs with rivets or solder.
7. File any remaining sharp edges and run a gloved hand over everything (H 157).

## Project 2: a ring of bent steel on a jig (H 138–140; W 59–60)

**Teaches:** repeatable bending.
1. Cut a plywood former to the ring's inside diameter and screw it to a sturdy table (H 138–140). Use ply at least **twice** the thickness of the steel, because the ends will overlap (W 60).
2. Clamp one end of the steel rod or flat bar to the former (H 138–140), or trap it with a screwed-down block (W 59).
3. Bend it around in small steps, hammering it tight to the former (H 138–140), until the ends overlap. Mark across both ends at the overlap and cut them (W 59).
4. Force the ends together on the jig and braze or weld it (H 138–140). The joint must be strong enough to hold the trapped spring-back, or the ring won't stay round off the former (W 60).
5. Alternative: bend round a slightly **undersized** former and let the spring-back open it to size (W 59).

## Project 3: a welded candle stand (H 134, 157–160; W 52–67)

**Teaches:** cutting, fit-up, tack welding, full welds, finishing.
1. Draw it, and make a cut list in mild steel rod, flat bar and a small plate (H 157–160). [General knowledge] for the design itself.
2. Cut with a portaband or chop saw; **deburr every cut** (H 134; W 54).
3. Clean off mill scale and grease (H 157–160), for example with washing-up liquid and a warm-water rinse (W 52).
4. Get ready to weld: clear flammables, screens and notices up, extinguisher and fire blanket at hand; boots, overalls, apron, gauntlets, mask; return clamp on the work near the joint; gas on at 10–15 L/min; set voltage and wire speed; trim the wire (W 64–65).
5. Clamp the parts square and **tack weld** each joint, then check it's square before full welds ([ch. 30](30-metal.md#technique-h-144148-w-6067)) (W 66–67).
6. Weld the joints fully (W 65).
7. Grind welds smooth where they show, **dull every edge**, degrease and **prime immediately** (H 157).
8. Paint it as iron, brass or bronze ([ch. 28 recipes](../4-fabrication-core/28-painting-and-finishing-recipes.md)).

## Project 4: a soldered joint (H 154–157)

1. Clean both parts with fine steel wool until bright.
2. Fit them tightly and clamp or wire them.
3. Apply the least aggressive flux that works.
4. **Heat the work, not the solder**, and let capillary action draw the solder in.
5. Don't move it until it's solid. Clean off active flux afterward.
6. Use **lead-free solder** and extract the fumes (H 155).

## Project 5: cold-bent semicircles and S-scrolls (W 56–60)

**Teaches:** spring-back, iterative former-making, two-person bending.
1. Draw the curve full size. Cut test blanks of flat or round bar well over length, with deburred ends (W 58).
2. **Semicircle:** mark a circle of the target radius on 18 mm ply with a line through the centre; cut it out and **screw** it to a strong bench. Screw down a block that traps one end of the steel tight against the disc (W 58–59).
3. Lay a metre of stout timber against the steel and roll it firmly round the former. Release and let it spring back (W 59).
4. Compare with the drawing, recut the disc a little smaller, refit it (keeping the trapping gap tight) and bend a fresh piece. Repeat until it matches. For a one-off, bend on a slightly small former and pull the ends open to fit (W 59).
5. **S-scroll:** trace both curves onto the bench and onto ply; cut a former for each and fix them so the steel is trapped between them with plenty of overlap. Two people bend both halves at once so the steel doesn't slip (W 59).
6. Adjust for spring-back by redrawing the curves with a flexible ruler and recutting, without touching the overlap zone; refit the formers in the same screw holes (W 59).
7. Mark the finished ends from the drawing and trim (W 59).

## Quick reference

**Aviation snips** (H 133–134): yellow straight, red left, green right. Capacity roughly 18-gauge aluminium, 24-gauge mild steel, 26-gauge stainless.

**Drilling speed** (H 137; W 55): bigger bits slower; aluminium fast, brass medium, steel slow. Center-punch first, clamp the work, and never drill steel dry. For HSS in mild steel, aim for about 30 m/min at the bit's edge: about 3,200 rpm for 3 mm, 800 rpm for 12 mm; lubricate with 10% soluble oil in water.

**MIG joint prep** (W 64): butt up to 2 mm; small gap or 30° bevels over 2 mm; 60° vee plus root gap over 5 mm. Shroud about 10 mm off the work (W 65).

**Common alloys** (H 157–160): mild steel 1018/1020 for most work; 5160 spring steel for stage-combat blades; aluminium 6061-T6 as the shop standard; 7075 dents too easily for stage blades. **Don't weld galvanized steel.**

**Soldering vs. brazing** (H 154–155): soldering is below 800°F, brazing above. Brazing can join dissimilar metals.
