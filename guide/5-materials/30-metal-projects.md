# Metal Projects (projects for ch. 30)

Starter projects and quick references for [ch. 30](30-metal.md). Hart recommends learning welding by building a small object rather than running practice beads (see the chapter's classroom prompts). Steps marked **[General knowledge]** aren't from the guide's sources. **Complete welding and grinder safety training first** ([ch. 30](30-metal.md#welding-safety-h-142144); [ch. 20](../4-fabrication-core/20-health-and-safety.md)).

## Project 1: a folded sheet-metal tray (H 140–141)

**Teaches:** patterning, cutting with snips, folding safe edges, riveting.
1. Pattern the tray in cardstock first, including tabs for the corners ([ch. 24](../4-fabrication-core/24-patterning.md)).
2. Trace onto thin aluminium or mild steel sheet.
3. Cut with aviation snips: **yellow = straight, red = left, green = right** (H 133–134).
4. **Fold every edge over** so no raw edge is exposed (H 140–141).
5. Fold the sides on a homemade **brake** (a clamp board and hinged bar) (H 140–141).
6. Join the corner tabs with rivets or solder.
7. File any remaining sharp edges and run a gloved hand over everything (H 157).

## Project 2: a ring of bent steel on a jig (H 138–140)

**Teaches:** repeatable bending.
1. Cut a plywood former to the ring's inside diameter and screw it to a sturdy table (H 138–140).
2. Clamp one end of the steel rod or flat bar to the former.
3. Bend it around in small steps, hammering it tight to the former.
4. Cut, close the gap, and braze or weld it.

## Project 3: a welded candle stand [General knowledge]

**Teaches:** cutting, fit-up, tack welding, full welds, finishing.
1. Draw it, and make a cut list in mild steel rod, flat bar and a small plate (H 157–160).
2. Cut with a portaband or chop saw; **deburr every cut** (H 134).
3. Clean off mill scale and grease (H 158).
4. Clamp the parts square and **tack weld** each joint, then check it's square before full welds ([ch. 30](30-metal.md#technique-h-144148)).
5. Weld the joints fully.
6. Grind welds smooth where they show, **dull every edge**, degrease and **prime immediately** (H 157).
7. Paint it as iron, brass or bronze ([ch. 28 recipes](../4-fabrication-core/28-painting-and-finishing-recipes.md)).

## Project 4: a soldered joint (H 154–157)

1. Clean both parts with fine steel wool until bright.
2. Fit them tightly and clamp or wire them.
3. Apply the least aggressive flux that works.
4. **Heat the work, not the solder**, and let capillary action draw the solder in.
5. Don't move it until it's solid. Clean off active flux afterward.
6. Use **lead-free solder** and extract the fumes (H 155).

## Quick reference

**Aviation snips** (H 133–134): yellow straight, red left, green right. Capacity roughly 18-gauge aluminium, 24-gauge mild steel, 26-gauge stainless.

**Drilling speed** (H 137): bigger bits slower; aluminium fast, brass medium, steel slow. Center-punch first, clamp the work, and never drill steel dry.

**Common alloys** (H 157–160): mild steel 1018/1020 for most work; 5160 spring steel for stage-combat blades; aluminium 6061-T6 as the shop standard; 7075 dents too easily for stage blades. **Don't weld galvanized steel.**

**Soldering vs. brazing** (H 154–155): soldering is below 800°F, brazing above. Brazing can join dissimilar metals.
