# Molding and Casting Projects (projects for ch. 38)

Starter projects and quick references for [ch. 38](38-molding-and-casting.md), cheap to advanced. Hart's first casting exercise is a plaster press mold (H 9). **Read each product's data sheet and SDS first**; polyurethanes in particular need serious ventilation (H 278–280). Steps marked **[General knowledge]** aren't from the guide's sources.

## Project 1: a plaster press mold (H 9, 271–274)

**Teaches:** plaster mixing, release, positives and negatives.
1. Roll out a slab of oil clay about 1" thick in a small box.
2. Press objects (shells, keys, buttons, leaves) into the clay to make impressions.
3. Mix plaster: water at 70–100°F in a flexible bucket; **sift plaster into the water**, let it slake 1–2 minutes, mix 2–5 minutes without whipping in air; tap and skim (H 271–274).
4. Pour into the box over the clay.
5. Demold when cool (about 15–30 minutes). The plaster now has raised positives of each object.
6. **Never pour plaster down a drain** (H 271–274).

## Project 2: a two-piece silicone box mold (H 260–265, 283–284)

**Teaches:** parting lines, registration keys, release between halves.
1. Seal a porous master ([ch. 38](38-molding-and-casting.md#release-agents-h-265267)). **No shellac under silicone** (H 267).
2. Embed the master halfway in an oil-clay bed at the seam line. Build walls around it.
3. Carve **registration keys** into the clay (H 260–265).
4. Mix silicone exactly to ratio, measuring each part in its own cup and combining in a third (H 267, 284).
5. Pour in a thin, high stream to one spot and let it flow (H 269–271).
6. When cured, flip it, remove the clay, and **apply release to the silicone**: silicone sticks to silicone (H 260–265).
7. Pour the second half. Cut a sprue and vents to the highest points (H 269–271).
8. Demold, and cast plaster, resin or more silicone in it.

## Project 3: a brush-up glove mold with a mother mold (H 260–265, 274)

**Teaches:** saving expensive rubber on big masters.
1. Brush a thin beauty coat of rubber over the master.
2. Build up thicker coats, colouring alternate layers to judge thickness.
3. Divide with clay **fences** for a multi-piece mold if needed.
4. Build a **mother mold** of plaster bandage over the rubber (H 274).
5. Demold the mother mold, then peel the rubber glove off the master.

## Project 4: casting a hand in alginate (H 286–289)

**Teaches:** life casting safely.
1. Fill a container with ½" clearance around the hand.
2. Mix alginate with warm water; the actor sits and holds the hand posed about 10 minutes.
3. The actor wiggles to break the suction and withdraws the hand.
4. **Cast immediately** (usually plaster), because alginate keeps shrinking. Don't make the actor come back.
5. **Never encase body parts in plaster** (H 290).

## Project 5: a quick silicone-caulk mold (H 284–285)

For a temporary, cheap mold: knead clear 100% silicone caulk with glycerin and a little acrylic paint, handling it with soapy gloves. Press it on in layers and let it cure 24–48 hours. Results are inconsistent, so use it only for short runs (H 284–285).

## Quick reference: release and mixing

**Resin into a plaster mold** (H 266, 279): seal the dry plaster (shellac, lacquer or paint) → wax → dust with cornstarch → PVA release.

**No release for absorption casting** in plaster: it clogs the pores (H 268–269).

**Two-part mixing** (H 267, 284): measure each part in its own cup, combine in a third; tare containers when weighing; mix exact ratios.

**Fighting bubbles** (H 269–271): sprue to the highest point; vents as high as the sprue; pour a thin, high stream to one spot; tap and vibrate; vacuum-degas viscous materials.

**Cure inhibitors for silicone** (H 283–284): sulfur clay, latex (even latex gloves), shellac.

**Shore hardness** (H 286): OO very soft, A flexible, D rigid. The scales don't convert.
