# 35. Leather

> **Sources:** H ch.11 (pp. 218–220), ch.15 (p. 293). Sections marked **[General knowledge]** aren't from the guide's sources.
> **[Check current]** Leather dyes, finishes and adhesives are reformulated often; check the SDS, especially for solvent-based dyes and cements.

Leather turns up in belts, holsters, scabbards, bags, book covers, armour, saddles and upholstery. It can be sewn like heavy fabric or, if vegetable-tanned, carved, stamped and moulded. Fabric sewing is in [ch. 34](34-fabric-and-upholstery.md); moulding leather into shells is in [ch. 36](36-shells-and-laminates.md).

## Leather (H 218–220)

- **Vegetable-tanned** leather is the only kind you can carve, stamp, emboss, hammer or mold ([ch. 36](36-shells-and-laminates.md)). Chrome-tanned garment and finished leathers can't be molded.
- Thickness is measured in **ounces** (1 oz ≈ 1/64"). **Tooling leather** runs 3–10 oz; 8–9 oz is the favorite.
- Cut with a knife and straightedge, a rotary cutter or heavy shears. **Skive** (thin) from the back.
- **Stitching tooling leather** is usually meant to show:
  1. Mark spacing with a stitch marker and pre-punch holes with a **diamond awl**.
  2. Cut a **stitching groove** on heavy leather.
  3. Glue the pieces together (barge or contact cement), then finish punching through.
  4. Use **waxed** sinew or linen thread and waxed needles.
- **Saddle stitch**: two needles on one thread, both passing through every hole from opposite sides. Backstitch two holes to finish; no knot needed. You may need **up to 7× the seam length** in thread.
- **Garment and upholstery leather** sews like fabric with adjustments: weights or clips instead of pins, leather needles, a walking foot, and **polyester or nylon thread** (cotton rots against leather). Seams often have to be stitched open.
- **Faux leather**: pleather, Naugahyde, bonded leather.
- **Fur**: real fur is worked like leather; faux fur like fabric. **Cut from the back with a blade** to avoid slicing the pile, then comb the fur over the seams. Shave faux fur in places for realism.

## Moulding leather (H 293)

Soak vegetable-tanned leather (15 minutes to 12 hours), drape it over a positive form, pin it in the trim areas (steel pins stain), and work it with a **sticketta** (a rounded wooden tool) for 20–60 minutes until it holds. Hammer in details as it dries, then burnish. It dries shoe-stiff and **breathes**, which makes it comfortable for masks and armour. For harder, more brittle **cuir bouilli**, dip it briefly (2 minutes or less) in boiling water or bake it low (120–175°F) (H 293). See [ch. 36](36-shells-and-laminates.md#water--and-heat-activated-sheets) for other shell materials.

## Tooling, dyeing and finishing [General knowledge]

- **Casing.** To tool or stamp vegetable-tanned leather, dampen it evenly with water (often with a sponge) and wait until the surface just starts to return to its natural colour. Tooling too wet or too dry gives poor impressions.
- **Tools.** A swivel knife cuts decorative lines; stamps and a mallet press patterns; edge bevelers round the edges; a slicker or burnisher polishes cut edges.
- **Dyes** come as spirit (alcohol) based, oil based and water based. Spirit dyes penetrate deeply but dry out leather and give off fumes: ventilate and wear gloves. Apply in thin coats with a dauber or airbrush. Test on scrap first.
- **Antiquing** gels and pastes settle into tooled lines to show off detail.
- **Finishes** (acrylic resolenes, waxes, oils) seal and protect. Use a flexible finish on anything that bends.

- **Hardware**: rivets, snaps, buckles, D-rings and grommets need the right setters. Buy setters that match the hardware size.
- **Faking leather.** For stage, painted fabric, vinyl, EVA foam with a pressed texture ([ch. 33](33-eva-foam.md)) or even paper can read as leather at a distance and cost far less.

## For the classroom

- **Leather sampler.** Students case, tool, stamp, dye, antique and finish a small panel, then sew one edge with a saddle stitch.
- **Small complete object.** Students make a simple belt pouch or book cover in tooling leather, from pattern to finish, in Hart's small-complete-object spirit (H 9).
- **Real vs. fake.** Compare real leather, painted vinyl and textured EVA from the front row and the back of the house, and price each.
