# Shells and Laminates Projects (projects for ch. 36)

Recipes and starter projects for [ch. 36](36-shells-and-laminates.md). Hart advises learning papier-mâché before fiberglass (H 9). Steps marked **[General knowledge]** aren't from the guide's sources.

## Recipe cards

| Recipe | Mix | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Wheat paste** | About 2 parts water : 1 part all-purpose flour. Whisk part of the water with the flour; boil the rest; stir in the slurry; boil 3–5 min; cool (H 297–301) | Keeps 2–3 days refrigerated |
| **PVA mâché binder** | White glue thinned with water (H 297–301) | Strong and flexible. Carpenter's glue is stiffer and sands better |
| **Paper pulp** | Blended paper + binder (H 297–301) | Sculpt features; final skim coat |
| **Felt stiffener** | PVA diluted up to 1 part water : 2 parts glue (H 292–293) | Soak industrial wool felt, wring out, mould like wet leather |

## Project 1: a papier-mâché bowl or mask (H 9, 297–301)

**Teaches:** layering, drying and sealing.
1. Build a form: a balloon, crumpled foil, a clay mould, or chicken wire.
2. Cover it with cling film or a release if you'll want it back ([ch. 38](38-molding-and-casting.md#release-agents-h-265267)).
3. **Tear** newsprint into strips along the grain (H 297–301).
4. Dip in paste, strip off the excess, and lay overlapping strips. **Alternate the direction of each layer**, as in plywood (H 297–301).
5. Build 4–6 layers, finishing with tissue or kraft for a smooth surface.
6. Air-dry until merely damp, then finish with low heat (100–150°F) or fans. Drying too fast too early cracks it (H 297–301).
7. Pop it loose while still green, trim, and seal with shellac or an acrylic or polyurethane emulsion (H 297–301).
8. Prime and paint.

## Project 2: a buckram crown (H 292) [General knowledge]

1. Pattern the crown in card.
2. Cut millinery buckram; sew **millinery wire** into the edges (H 292).
3. Soak it in warm water, shape it around a form, and let it dry stiff.
4. Stiffen further with more layers or PVA.
5. Decorate with jewels, trim and metal leaf ([ch. 28 recipes](../4-fabrication-core/28-painting-and-finishing-recipes.md)).

## Project 3: a cloth-mâché drape or shell (H 301)

1. Cut muslin or cheesecloth **on the bias** for strength (H 301).
2. Soak in PVA or a theatrical emulsion and drape over the form or pin into folds.
3. Alternate layer directions. Let dry and seal.

## Project 4: a water-based composite shell (H 302–305)

Hart recommends water-based resins for beginners, because they're far less toxic than polyester (H 303). Sanding dust is still hazardous: follow the product's instructions, wear gloves, goggles and a respirator, and extract dust.
1. Seal and release the mould or form.
2. Brush on a thickened first coat to capture detail.
3. Lay pre-cut pieces of glass cloth, no bigger than a sheet of paper, wetting each until it turns transparent. Wear gloves even while cutting (H 303–305).
4. Lay the next layer crosswise over the previous seams. Two layers suffice for most hand props (H 303–305).
5. Demold at the green stage, trim, and **seal every edge**: protruding strands are like needles (H 305–306).

## Quick reference

**Papier-mâché vs. fiberglass** (H 299, 302): a well-sealed mâché prop can outlast fiberglass because it flexes under impact instead of shattering.

**Polyester resin conditions** (H 303): 60–85°F and under 60% relative humidity. Petroleum jelly inhibits its surface cure (H 303–305).
