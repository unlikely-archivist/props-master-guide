# Foam Projects (projects for ch. 32)

Starter projects for [ch. 32](32-foams-and-rubbers.md). EVA foam builds have their own file: [EVA Foam Build Walkthroughs](33-eva-foam-projects.md). Food built from foam is in the [Prop Food Recipe Cards](41-prop-food-recipes.md). Steps marked **[General knowledge]** aren't from the guide's sources.

> **Safety:** hot-wiring or hot-knifing polystyrene releases styrene vapour; rasping makes dust. Ventilate, use an organic-vapour respirator per the SDS for hot cutting, and a particulate mask and goggles for rasping (H 243; M 143–144).

## Project 1: a carved and coated XPS rock or gravestone

**Teaches:** laminating, carving, hard-coating, painting.
1. **Laminate** 1" or 2" XPS sheets into a block with water-based contact cement ("green glue") or wood glue. Don't use solvent sprays: they dissolve XPS (H 252–253; FF 26–29, 157).
2. Clamp with cauls or a strap over boards so the clamps don't dent the foam (FF 11, 125, 157).
3. Draw the outline on two faces. Rough-cut with a hot-wire cutter, band saw or pull saw ([ch. 37](37-sculpting.md#subtractive-techniques-h-242243-w-132136)).
4. Shape with a Surform or rasp; a rasped face hides glue lines (FF 146).
5. **Coat it** so it survives handling: scenic dope or joint compound with PVA for a hard shell, or cheesecloth-mâché for strength ([ch. 27](../4-fabrication-core/27-surfaces-and-texture.md#coating-foam-h-315317); [ch. 27 recipes](../4-fabrication-core/27-surfaces-and-texture-recipes.md)).
6. Base-coat, wash and drybrush to look like stone ([ch. 28 recipes](../4-fabrication-core/28-painting-and-finishing-recipes.md)).

## Project 2: a slice-method foam figure (H 243–244)

1. Cut front and side profiles from plywood and set them on a frame.
2. Add top-profile "slices" between them.
3. Fill the spaces with pre-roughed foam blocks.
4. Carve flush to the slices ("connect the dots").
5. Coat and paint. Hart built a three-foot garden gnome this way (H 243–244).

## Project 3: backer-rod molding and trim (H 179–181)

1. Halve polyethylene **backer rod** lengthwise with a sharp blade.
2. Glue it to a frame or picture surround as curved molding (H 179–181).
3. Prime with a flexible primer and paint ([ch. 28](../4-fabrication-core/28-painting-and-finishing.md)).

## Project 4: an upholstered foam cushion [General knowledge]

1. Trace the base onto upholstery foam and cut it with an electric carving knife, held vertical (H 212–217).
2. Glue a layer of batting over the top with foam spray adhesive.
3. Cover it: see the boxed-cushion and drop-seat projects in [Fabric and Upholstery Projects](34-fabric-and-upholstery-projects.md).

## Quick reference

**Which glue on which foam** (H 179–181, 252–253; FF 26–29; KF 7, 15):

| Foam | Good glues | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| XPS / EPS | Water-based contact cement, PVA, wood glue, low-temperature hot glue | Solvent sprays, solvent contact cement, polyester resin |
| EVA | Contact cement (KF's choice); hot glue (Hart's choice) | — (see the [disagreement](33-eva-foam.md#where-the-sources-disagree)) |
| Upholstery foam | Contact or barge cement, foam spray adhesive | — |
| Polyethylene (backer rod, Ethafoam) | Hot glue, contact cement | Most water-based glues won't grip |
