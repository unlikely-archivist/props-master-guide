# Fabric and Upholstery Projects (projects for ch. 34)

Starter projects for [ch. 34](34-fabric-and-upholstery.md), following Hart's sequence for learning by finishing small objects: a pillowcase for sewing, then a recovered drop seat for upholstery (H 9). Steps marked **[General knowledge]** aren't from the guide's sources.

## Project 1: a pillowcase (H 9) [General knowledge]

**Teaches:** cutting, straight seams, hems, pressing.
1. Measure a pillow and add seam allowance (¼–⅝") and a hem allowance (H 201–212).
2. Mark on the **wrong side** and cut flat on the table with fabric-only scissors (H 201–212).
3. Sew the side and bottom seams **right sides together** (H 201–212).
4. Finish the raw edges with a serger, zigzag or a French seam (H 202–205, 210–212).
5. Turn and press a double-turned hem at the opening (H 210–212).
6. Turn right side out, push the corners out, and press.

## Project 2: a boxed cushion with piping (H 202–217; W 153)

**Teaches:** boxing strips, piping, zips or slip-stitch closures.
1. Cut a foam insert ([Foam Projects](32-foams-and-rubbers-projects.md)) and wrap it in batting (W 147). [General knowledge] for steps 1–2 and 4–6 beyond the cited points.
2. Cut a top, a bottom and a boxing strip, plus bias strips for piping.
3. Make **piping**: cut bias strips (at 45° to the weave) wide enough to go round the cord plus 25 mm, join them end to end and press the joins, then wrap the cord and sew as close to it as possible with a piping or zipper foot (H 215–217; W 153).
4. Sew piping around the top and bottom panels.
5. Sew the boxing strip between them, clipping and notching at the corners (H 202–205).
6. Leave one side open; insert the cushion and close with a **slip (ladder) stitch** (H 206–210).

## Project 3: a recovered drop seat (H 9, 212–217; W 148)

**Teaches:** the upholstery sandwich and stapling order.
1. Remove the seat; strip old fabric if needed.
2. Build the sandwich: **plywood base → polyurethane foam traced from the base → batting → optional muslin → upholstery fabric** (H 212–217). Keep foam and wadding from wrapping onto the underside of the base; cut the fabric with at least 5–6 cm to grip under the base, and check the pile or pattern direction (W 148).
3. Mark the centre of each side on the base and the fabric.
4. Staple **centre front, centre back, centre right, centre left**, then work out toward the corners with even tension (H 212–217).
5. **Fold, trim and miter** each corner, keeping pleats close together (H 212–217), or make a single pleat on one side of each corner or box pleats meeting at the corner; slipstitch obtrusive pleats down (W 148).
6. Staple a **cambric** dust cover underneath (H 212–217).
7. Refit the seat.

## Project 4: a deep-buttoned panel (H 215–217; W 155)

**Prepare the filling** (W 155):
1. Bevel the back edges of foam about **half the height of the diamonds** and glue it to the ply. If pulling down with calico now, use strips glued along the edges, leaving the buttoned area clear.
2. Mark the button positions on the foam; measure the distances between buttons and to the edges, and **record them on a diagram**.
3. Cut a hole about twice the button's size down to the ply at each point with a length of thin-walled steel tube sharpened on a grinder or belt sander, pushed in and twisted. Make shallow knife cuts between buttons where the pleats will go.
4. Drill through the ply at the centre of each hole.
5. Lay polyester wadding over the foam and work it into the holes and cuts with your fingers.

**Mark the cover** (W 155):
6. Add about **40%** to every spacing on the diagram, and the same allowance to each margin.
7. Lay the cover face down, chalk the centreline, set out the enlarged positions, and push an awl through each so it shows on the right side.

**Assemble** (W 155):
8. Lay the cover on. Thread a button onto a generous doubled length of twine, pass both ends through a long needle, and starting on the centreline, push it through a button mark, the filling and the ply hole. Tie the ends round a rolled-up fabric tuft with a **slip knot**.
9. Repeat, working outward from the middle.
10. Tighten the knots to pull every button about halfway in. Arrange the pleats between buttons to face **downward on backs, outward on seats**, neatening them with a mixing stick or table knife.
11. Form the horizontal and vertical pleats radiating from the outer buttons, pull them down firmly, and fix them with skewers or temporary tacks; then pull down and fix the fabric between them. When tension and pleats are right, staple permanently.
12. Pull the buttons to final depth, check they're even, and tie off with **half hitches**. Neaten the pleats, trim the surplus, and cover the staples with trim if needed.

## Project 5: a bordered (feather-edged) seat (W 152–153)

1. Build the seat with a firmer foam strip round the edge to make the **feather edge**, a continuous top sheet, and thin wadding over it (W 146–147).
2. Make a template of the seat's plan view, add a small seam allowance, and cut the seat piece (W 152).
3. Cut a border strip, joining pieces if needed, with extra length and depth to grip when pulling down (W 152).
4. Pin the border to the seat piece and sew, inserting piping if wanted (W 152).
5. Turn it right side out, pull it down over the seat, and fix it to the frame. Sized to the foam, with thin wadding over, it should fit snugly (W 152).
6. On complex shapes, join in place instead: glue a 4–5 cm calico strip round the top edge of the border area, pin the seat fabric to it and hand-stitch; then turn and press the border's top edge, pin it and slipstitch it to the seat cover before pulling it down (W 152–153).

## Quick reference

**Seams** (H 202–205): plain; flat-felled (strong, finished both sides); French (encloses raw edges, for sheers).

**Hand stitches** (H 206–210): running/basting; back stitch (strongest); whip/overcast; catch stitch (invisible hems); slip/ladder stitch (invisible closure).

**Machine tension** (H 206–207): too tight puckers or breaks the thread; too loose makes a bird's nest underneath. Test on a scrap of the same fabric.
