# Paper and Graphics Projects (projects for ch. 39)

Starter projects for [ch. 39](39-paper-and-graphics.md). Steps marked **[General knowledge]** aren't from the guide's sources. Check copyright and trademark clearance before reproducing real images, logos or brands ([ch. 39](39-paper-and-graphics.md#copyright-and-trademarks-h-355)).

## Project 1: aged parchment (M 158)

Mussman's method:
1. Crumple the paper, dampen it and tear the edges.
2. Flatten it and soak it in strong tea.
3. Dry it and iron it flat.

A quicker alternative is a spritz of Design Master Glossy Wood Tone (M 158).

## Project 2: a period letter (H 353–360)

**Teaches:** research, handwriting, ageing, consumables.
1. Research the period's paper size, writing tool and letter conventions ([ch. 7](../2-admin/07-research-and-period-accuracy.md)).
2. Write the content, or use the script's text. Handwriting should suit the character (H 353).
3. Age the paper with a tea or coffee bath; dry in a ~200°F oven, a microwave or with a hair dryer (H 357–358). Add blooms with instant-coffee crystals (H 358–360).
4. Write or print the letter. **Inks may run** if you age after writing; writing on aged paper can look newer than the paper (H 360).
5. **Don't burn the edges** unless the letter was in a fire (H 360).
6. Make **multiples** if the letter is torn, burned or crumpled each night (M 8).

## Project 3: a period newspaper (H 355–357) [General knowledge]

1. Research a real newspaper from the period for layout, fonts, column widths and ad style.
2. Lay out the front page in graphics software with **fictional or public-domain content**. Put the lines the actor reads on the visible side.
3. Print on a wide-format printer or at a print shop on newsprint-weight paper (H 355–356).
4. Fold it and age it lightly if needed.

## Project 4: a "vintage" jar label (H 357, 359)

1. Scan a modern label, or design one.
2. Alter it in software to suit the period.
3. Print it on a laser printer (waterproof toner) or inkjet then seal it.
4. Adhere it to a real jar. Hart made a "vintage" rubber-cement jar this way (H 359).
5. For bottles washed nightly, protect labels with clear packing tape (H 72).

## Project 5: a fake book (H 356)

1. Choose a real book of the right size.
2. Print a new cover and wrap it.
3. If the actor flips through it, the inside must match: no colour photos in a period text, and poetry shouldn't look like prose (H 356).

## Project 6: a waterslide decal (H 357)

1. Print the design in reverse if needed, on laser waterslide paper (inkjet ink washes off in the soak).
2. Seal it if the paper requires it.
3. Soak it, slide it onto a primed surface, and squeegee out the water.
4. Seal it with a clear coat.

## Quick reference

**Printers** (H 355–356): inkjet smears when wet; laser toner is waterproof and prints on acetate; wide-format plotters for newspapers and printed "paintings."

**Paper and heat** (H 357–358): dry aged paper at around 200°F; paper ignites near 451°F and smoulders before that.

**Prop money**: check current law before producing realistic currency **[Check current]**.
