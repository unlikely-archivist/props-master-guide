# 15. Shopping

> **Sources:** M ch.6 (pp. 78–83), ch.8 (pp. 115–118); H ch.19 (pp. 363–364); W ch.2 (p. 22), ch.3 (p. 51), ch.7 (p. 125). Sections marked **[General knowledge]** aren't from the guide's sources.
> **[Check current]** Stores, online marketplaces, delivery times and purchasing rules change constantly. Keep your own vendor list current.

Buying is the fastest route to many props, and the easiest to waste money on. Decide first whether to buy at all ([ch. 11](../2-admin/11-buy-source-or-build.md)), then shop with a plan.

## Buy (M 115–118)

Good places to shop:
- antique stores
- resale and thrift stores
- dollar stores
- craft and hardware stores
- discount retailers
- online

Buying tips (M 117):
- Take time to hunt for bargains, and resist impulse buys.
- Choose generic over name brand and used over new.
- **Check return policies.** Yard sales, auctions and many thrift shops don't take returns. Buying unusable props with an unreturnable budget is the worst outcome.
- When time is short, sometimes you go with your gut. Just be sure it's usable (M 118).

| Finishes, cleaners | Antique restorers, shoe-repair shops |
| Mineral oil, glycerin, acetone, alcohol, plaster bandage | Pharmacies |
| Foil, gelatin, containers | Grocery stores |
| Corrugated plastic | Sign shops |
| Hot-melt vinyl | Fishing-supply stores |
| Theatre-specific goods | Theatrical suppliers (mainly in larger cities) |

Wilson's supplier notes (W 22, 51, 125): ask steel suppliers for a **stock list** and phone for quotes (minimum orders and free-delivery thresholds apply); get **moulding profile charts** from timber merchants and specialist moulding firms that serve theatre and film, so designers can choose; some firms sell ready-made **furniture parts** such as cabriole legs and turned columns; polystyrene suppliers **slice blocks to order** and may cut cylinders and spheres. When researching a material, work out **who else uses it**: boat-building books cover epoxy in detail, model-figure books cover white-metal casting (W 185).

Manufacturers will usually tell you which retailers stock their products. Weigh shipping costs and delivery time against driving time. **Keep a vendor list** (H 364) and a contacts list: antique shops, area prop masters, designers, rental houses, repair shops, other theatres and freelancers (M 83).

### Catalogs and references (M 78–82)
Keep a working library of how-to and reference books (furniture styles, period ads, the 1897 Sears catalog reprint, weapons, bottles, coins and so on) and a **research file** of clippings, photos and instructions from past shows. If you keep supplier catalogs, **don't order from outdated ones**, and respect copyright when reproducing images. For organizing a research file and checking dates, see [ch. 7](../2-admin/07-research-and-period-accuracy.md#building-a-research-file).

## Shopping in practice [General knowledge]

**Before you go**
- Shop from the **prop list and cost breakdown**, with measurements, reference images and the designer's priorities on your phone ([ch. 6](../2-admin/06-script-breakdown-and-prop-list.md), [ch. 8](../2-admin/08-budgets-and-paperwork.md)).
- Know your **payment method and limits** (petty cash, company card, purchase order) and whether your institution is **tax-exempt**. Carry the exemption certificate if it is (M 39).
- **Batch trips** by area and plan the route (M 106). Bring a tape measure, swatches, a camera, bags and padding, and a vehicle that fits the largest item.
- Book a second person for heavy or awkward items.

**In the store**
- **Photograph options** and send them to the designer for approval before buying, when time allows (M 119).
- Check **size, scale and sightline** against the set, not just the look. Measure everything.
- Check **condition**: wobbly joints, missing parts, smells, pests in upholstered thrift furniture, and broken wiring in lamps.
- **Buy for the run.** If the prop will break, be eaten or wear out, buy multiples now from the same batch so they match.
- **Ask for "approval" or "on hold" terms** at independent shops. Many will let you take an item to show the designer, or hold it for a day.

**Online**
- Allow for **shipping time and returns**. Rush shipping can cost more than the item.
- Photos lie about colour and scale. Read dimensions, not pictures.
- Keep order confirmations with your receipts.

**After**
- **Keep every receipt** and log it against the right budget line the same day ([ch. 8](../2-admin/08-budgets-and-paperwork.md#paperwork-and-accounting-m-39-8391)).
- Note **return deadlines** for anything you might return if it's cut.
- Add good shops to your **vendor list**, with what they're good for and who to ask for.

## For the classroom

- **$50 challenge.** Students source five props for a given period scene with $50 and one afternoon, then present receipts, photos and what they'd change.
- **Vendor map.** The class builds a shared vendor list for your area: thrift, antique, hardware, fabric, specialty, and online, with notes on return policies and what each is best for.
- **Online vs. in person.** Students price one prop online (with shipping and delivery date) and in local stores (with travel time), and compare the true cost.
