# 17. Borrowing

> **Sources:** M ch.4 (pp. 39–40), ch.8 (pp. 112–115). Sections marked **[General knowledge]** aren't from the guide's sources.

Borrowing costs little money but a lot of trust. Done well, it builds relationships that will find props for years. Done badly, it closes doors for your whole theatre.

## Borrow (M 112–113)

Borrowing builds relationships with schools, other theatres, businesses and private lenders, and those relationships grow over time.

- **Barter** where your institution allows it, with comp tickets or a program credit (M 112).
- **Thank lenders**: a sincere note on letterhead, and a program acknowledgment if marketing agrees and you ask before the print deadline (M 113).
- **Use an agreement every time**, even with friends, and follow the same etiquette as for rentals: if it can't come back in the same condition, don't borrow it; if it breaks, tell the owner and make it right (M 114–115). See the etiquette and agreement sections in [ch. 16](16-renting.md#borrowing-and-renting-etiquette-m-114115).

## Who lends [General knowledge]

- **Other theatres and schools**: the most common lenders. Many swap freely with nearby companies; return the favour.
- **Local businesses**: antique shops, furniture stores, florists, restaurants, music shops and car dealers may lend in exchange for a program credit or comps.
- **Private lenders**: staff, board members, audience members and their families. Ask through your marketing or development office as well as your own network.
- **Specialists and collectors**: re-enactors, historical societies and hobby clubs often own accurate period items and will advise even if they won't lend.

## Borrowing well [General knowledge]

- **Ask clearly**: what the item is for, how it will be used, how long you need it, and how you will protect it.
- **Be honest about risk.** If an item will be handled roughly, dropped, eaten off or lit, say so, or don't borrow it.
- **Photograph condition** at pickup and return, as for rentals.
- **Insurance.** Ask whether your institution's insurance covers borrowed items, and for how much. Don't borrow anything valuable you couldn't replace.
- **Keep a borrowed-items list** for stage management (M 114–115) and return items **promptly** after closing, clean, with a thank-you note.
- **Track favours.** Keep notes on who lent what, so you can thank them again, credit them, and lend back.

## Key terms

lender · loan agreement · barter · program credit · comps · borrowed-items list

## For the classroom

- **The ask.** Students role-play asking a local business owner to lend an antique, then receiving it, and then telling them it was damaged.
- **Lender network.** The class drafts a list of potential lenders in your community, with what each might have and what you could offer in return.
