# 19. Recycling, Reusing and Building Stock

> **Sources:** M ch.1 (pp. 13–14), ch.5 (pp. 73–77), ch.9 (pp. 139–140); H ch.5 (p. 61). Sections marked **[General knowledge]** aren't from the guide's sources.

A props department's stock is its biggest asset: everything you keep is something you don't have to buy or build again. This chapter covers what a stock should hold, how to reuse and recycle, and how to cut waste. Storage, inventory and records are in [ch. 13](../2-admin/13-strike-storage-and-records.md).

## A basic prop stock

Mussman lists the kinds of items a healthy theatre stock should hold (M 13–14). Broadly:

- Furniture of many periods
- Linens
- Dishes and glassware
- Lamps and lanterns
- Luggage
- Phones and typewriters
- Books
- Foliage
- Kitchen and bar equipment
- Musical instruments
- Weapons
- Smoking supplies
- Toys and games

See [ch. 13](../2-admin/13-strike-storage-and-records.md) for what to keep and how to organize it.

## Reusing and transforming stock [General knowledge]

- **Design for reuse.** When you build, think about the next show: neutral finishes under show-specific paint, removable details, standard sizes, and joints that come apart for storage.
- **Re-skin rather than rebuild.** A stock chair can be repainted, recovered or re-trimmed for many periods ([ch. 18](18-pulling-altering-and-kitbashing.md)).
- **Keep parts.** Hart's found-object parts bin of hardware, lamp parts, trim and toy parts turns into the details of future props (H 61; [ch. 21](../4-fabrication-core/21-shop-space-and-tools.md#shop-supply-stock-m-5055-h-61)).
- **Know when to let go.** Mussman's guidance on what to keep and what to cull is in [ch. 13](../2-admin/13-strike-storage-and-records.md#what-to-keep-m-74). Broken, unsafe, moldy or never-used items cost storage space every year.

## Recycling and waste reduction [General knowledge]

- **Strike thoughtfully.** Separate reusable materials (lumber, hardware, fabric, foam offcuts) from waste at strike ([ch. 13](../2-admin/13-strike-storage-and-records.md#strike-m-139140)).
- **Share with other theatres and schools.** Many areas have prop and scenery swaps, reuse centres or online groups for passing on stock.
- **Donate** usable furniture and dressing to charity shops or reuse centres, and usable leftover paint to community programmes (H 344–345).
- **Dispose of hazardous waste properly.** Paints, solvents, resins and contaminated materials can't go in ordinary trash or drains ([ch. 20](../4-fabrication-core/20-health-and-safety.md#disposal-h-36-also-h-274-344345)).
- **Choose materials with an afterlife.** Where the needs allow, prefer materials that can be reused, recycled or safely disposed of. For example, some foams and plastics can't be recycled at all, while plain lumber and steel can. Hart's needs-and-means method ([ch. 22](../4-fabrication-core/22-the-build-method.md)) can include environmental cost as one of the means.
- **Cut consumable waste.** FF's fake food lasts for years if stored well, replacing real food bought for every show (FF 4, 22; [ch. 41](../5-materials/41-prop-food.md)).

## For the classroom

- **Stock audit.** Students audit one section of your storage: what's there, what's been used in the past five years, what's broken, and what should be culled, repaired or kept.
- **Design for the next show.** For a prop they're building, students describe how it could be reused in two future productions and change one design choice to make that easier.
- **Waste log.** During one build and strike, the class weighs or counts what goes to reuse, recycling and landfill, then proposes three changes for the next show.
