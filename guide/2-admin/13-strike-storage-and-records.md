# 13. Strike, Storage, Inventory and Records

> **Sources:** M ch.5 (pp. 71–77), ch.6 (pp. 78–83), ch.9 (pp. 139–140); H ch.2 (p. 20), ch.14 (p. 290)

## Strike (M 139–140)

**Strike** happens after closing, either immediately or within a few days. The prop master assembles the prop strike crew and equips it with the tool kit, several screw guns, and plenty of boxes, crates and laundry baskets.

- **Coordinate a few days ahead** with the technical director and master electrician. Props and electrics often start an hour or two before the carpenters and can clear most props off the set in that window.
- Agree on a **staging area** at the theatre and on who uses the shared vehicle when.
- **Work safely.** People get careless at strike, but props and scenery weigh just as much as they did at load-in.

## Returns (M 140)

- **Schedule returns at least a week before strike.**
- **Return items in the same condition (or better)** as they were lent: clean, repaired, and with any distressing removed.
- Return the right items to the right owner **in one trip**. Calling a lender repeatedly because you've "found another one" isn't acceptable.
- Clean everything going back into your own stock (wash dishes and linens, remove distressing paint) and put it back in its proper section promptly.
- Throw out show-specific items that won't be reused (specialized paper props, food) and anything damaged beyond repair.
- **Close the budget** (see [ch. 8](08-budgets-and-paperwork.md#paperwork-and-accounting-m-39-8391)).

## Prop storage (M 73–77)

### The ideal vs. reality
The dream is a climate-controlled, dust- and pest-free, well-lit warehouse with 8-foot shelving, rolling ladders and cantilever racks for furniture. Reality is usually found space (barns, lofts, rented units), with small props shelved in enclosed rooms and furniture in larger, rougher spaces (M 73).

### Enemies of stock (M 73–74)
- **Heat and humidity** warp wood, dull finishes, and leave fabrics damp, leading to **mold and mildew**.
- **Pests**: mice nest in cushions, birds soil furniture, moths eat fabric. A pest-control contract is worth paying for.

### What to keep (M 74)
- Props likely to be reused or rented out.
- **Hard-to-find** antiques and vintage items.
- **Well-built, classic** pieces that can play many periods.

What to **discard**: show-specific builds no one will reuse or rent. Avoid **overstocking modern furniture** if you rarely do contemporary shows. Fight your inner pack rat.

Hart adds a **sustainability** lens (H 20). Props already reuses and repurposes heavily, but before building, ask what happens afterward: Is it worth storing? Can the materials be reused? Would someone else want it? If you plan to reuse the materials, **design it to come apart**.

### Organizing (M 74–76)
- **Group like with like** and put related groups near each other (luggage next to bags, dish sets with drinkware, all soft goods together).
- Use metal shelving, clear bins, drawer units and dividers.
- Mussman's starter categories:

| | | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| antique tools | appliances | artificial flowers/foliage | bags |
| baskets | books & magazines | dish sets | drinkware |
| electronics | fake food | farm equipment | frames |
| gardening | glassware | kitchenware | knickknacks |
| lighting | medical/scientific | musical instruments | office supplies |
| paper props | personal accessories | religious items | restaurant/bar |
| rugs | serving pieces | smoking accessories | soft goods |
| sporting goods | toiletries | toys/games | vases |
| wall décor | weapons (locked) | miscellaneous | |

- **Furniture**:
  - Build heavy-duty platforms so more furniture fits underneath.
  - Remove table legs for storage.
  - Hang chairs on **bicycle hooks**.
  - Don't stack heavy items on upholstery, because foam keeps the dents (M 75–76).

### Inventory (M 76)
Work section by section. For each item, record a **brief description, dimensions where useful, and a digital photo**, and update it with every new acquisition. A good inventory lets you send stock options to directors, designers and other theatres. **[Suggestion]** Modern teams often keep this in a shared spreadsheet or database with photos rather than on paper.

### Access rules (M 76–77)
Props "grow legs" when people borrow them without asking. To prevent this:
- Lock storage if possible.
- Define who has access (the prop shop, the PM, and perhaps education staff).
- Provide **check-out forms** that in-house borrowers can fill in themselves ([Appendix B](../../appendices/B-paperwork-templates.md#6-in-house-prop-check-out)).
- If items keep disappearing, tell your supervisor and look for more secure storage.

Schedule regular cleaning and reorganizing, for example at the start and end of each season or after each strike.

## Tool inventory and kits (M 70–72)

- **Label every tool** ("PROP SHOP") as soon as you get it. Branded tools are less likely to wander off.
- Keep a **tool inventory** and review it yearly to plan replacements.
- **Personal kit** carried by most technicians: a small flashlight, a multi-tool, a 5–15 ft tape measure, a notepad and pen, a copy of the prop list, and a phone.
- **Transportable tool kit** for load-ins, especially from off-site shops. Ideally duplicate shop tools so you don't have to haul the originals back and forth. Tape a **laminated supply list** inside the lid so restocking is quick. A full list is in [Appendix B](../../appendices/B-paperwork-templates.md#11-transportable-tool-kit). Add a bucket of show-specific supplies.

## Files and collections (M 78–83)

- **Personal library**: how-to books (furniture building, upholstery, metalwork, scenic art, effects, molding and casting) and reference books (furniture history, period ads and catalogs, coins, stamps, bottles, weapons, food and drink). Grow it through your budget, book drives, resale shops and grants through your development office. Label books you own personally.
- **Research collection**: binders or digital folders of clippings, photos, instructions and past-show documentation.
- **Contacts list**: stores, rental houses, theatres, artisans and repair shops.
- **Company paperwork**: see [ch. 8](08-budgets-and-paperwork.md#paperwork-and-accounting-m-39-8391).
- **Archive prop bibles** for future reference (H 9).

## Mold archives (H 290)

If you keep molds, store them in **sealed plastic tubs**, without stacking. Flexible molds deform under weight, and rigid ones chip against each other. Moisture, dust, sunlight and even air degrade molds over time.

## For the classroom

- **Inventory project.** Students inventory one section of the department's actual prop storage (description, dimensions, photo, location) into a shared spreadsheet. This is real service to the program and a lesson in organization.
- **Keep/toss debate.** After a production, students decide what goes back to stock, what's rented out, what's recycled and what's discarded, and justify each call.
- **Strike plan.** Students write a strike and returns plan for a show with five borrowed items, two rentals and a co-producing partner.
