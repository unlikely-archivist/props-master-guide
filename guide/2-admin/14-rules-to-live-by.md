# 14. Rules to Live By

> **Sources:** gathered from M, H, J, FF, KF and B; each rule is cited where it's discussed in full. Rules marked **[General knowledge]** are widely shared prop-shop wisdom rather than points from the guide's sources.

Each source has a handful of rules its author keeps coming back to. This chapter collects them in one place, with a link to where each is explained. Read it before your first show and again before your fiftieth.

## The story and the actor

1. **The prop serves the story.** Ask whether every detail reads and whether it moves the story forward (B 25–26, 47; [ch. 4](../1-intro/04-theory-and-philosophy.md)).
2. **The actor comes first.** Almost every prop interacts with a body. "Can someone actually use it?" (B 57). Rehearsal props should match the size and weight of the actuals (H 2; M 120–121).
3. **Never ask an actor to perform an effect you haven't tested yourself** (M 149; [ch. 46](../7-effects/46-effects-and-breakaways.md)).
4. **Once actors have rehearsed with something, changing it costs them.** Small changes can throw off timing (M 12–13).

## Communication

5. **Never assume who owns an ambiguous item.** Raise it and get it assigned in writing (H 4; [ch. 1](../1-intro/01-what-is-a-prop.md)).
6. **Raise your red flag early.** If money, time or know-how won't stretch, say so as soon as you know, and bring alternatives (M 107–108; [ch. 10](10-working-with-other-departments.md#raise-your-red-flag-early-m-107108-127-137)).
7. **Never use constraints as an excuse.** If you truly can't do something, explain why and offer options (M 107).
8. **Read the whole rehearsal report**, not just the props section (M 124; [ch. 10](10-working-with-other-departments.md)).
9. **Ignore the published prop list.** It documents someone else's production (M 94–95; [ch. 6](06-script-breakdown-and-prop-list.md#ignore-the-published-prop-list-m-9495)).
10. **The prop list is a living document**, final only on opening night (M 100).
11. **Tell stage management about every change** after load-in (M 134).

## Money and time

12. **Fast, cheap, good: pick two** (H 362; [ch. 8](08-budgets-and-paperwork.md#the-project-triangle-h-362)).
13. **Performance dates never change because props are late** (M 106; [ch. 9](09-staffing-and-scheduling.md)).
14. **Keep a work journal**, and estimate at your own speed, not someone else's (H 364–366).
15. **Build in materials you know** when time is short. Experiment only when there's a backup plan (H 20, 366).
16. **Check return policies before you buy** (M 117; [ch. 15](../3-sourcing/15-shopping.md)).
17. **Make sure a fancy method or gadget actually saves something.** Berls's bevelled platform legs cost more than $700 "for a preference" (B 115).

## Other people's things

18. **If it can't come back in the same condition, buy or build instead** (M 114; [ch. 16](../3-sourcing/16-renting.md)).
19. **If you break it, tell the owner** and offer to make it right (M 114–115).
20. **Thank every lender**, in writing (M 113, 23–25).
21. **Don't permanently alter valuable stock** you use often (M 119).
22. **Don't touch props that aren't yours** (M 23–25).

## In the shop

23. **Safety is infrastructure, not an afterthought.** Read the SDS, use the right PPE, and follow your institution's rules ([ch. 20](../4-fabrication-core/20-health-and-safety.md)).
24. **When in doubt, vent it out – safely** (B 15). Don't trust your nose to judge fumes; ask your safety office (B 118).
25. **Experienced artisans spend more time measuring, laying out and building jigs; novices jump in with the saw** (H 97; [ch. 23](../4-fabrication-core/23-drawings-measuring-and-layout.md#jigs-h-9799)).
26. **Time spent on reference is time well spent** (KF 10; [ch. 33](../5-materials/33-eva-foam.md)).
27. **Compression beats fasteners.** Let loads travel through material, not screws (H 184; [ch. 26](../4-fabrication-core/26-structure-and-armatures.md#compression-beats-fasteners-h-184)).
28. **Plan your failure points** so the cheap part breaks first (H 189–190).
29. **Don't skip grits** when sanding (H 308; [ch. 27](../4-fabrication-core/27-surfaces-and-texture.md)).
30. **Realism comes from layers of translucent glaze**, not one opaque coat (FF 130, 157; H 335–339).
31. **Learn a craft by finishing one small, complete object**, not by drilling exercises (H 9).
32. **"Invented" doesn't mean "in use."** Check what your characters would really have had (J 9–10; [ch. 7](07-research-and-period-accuracy.md)).

## Onstage

33. **Clean every stock prop before it goes into rehearsal** (M 51).
34. **Secure anything that could tip, slide or fall** (M 131–132; [ch. 12](12-rehearsals-tech-and-performance.md#securing-props-m-131132)).
35. **Walk the house and "hide the uglies"** from the front row and the balcony before the audience arrives (M 132–133).
36. **Lock up every weapon, real or not, every night** (M 148–149; [ch. 42](../6-weapons/42-weapons-safety-and-policy.md)).
37. **Consumable food is fresh every performance** (M 8; [ch. 47](../7-effects/47-eating-drinking-and-smoking.md)).

## Yourself

38. **Praise publicly, critique privately** (M 19–20).
39. **Keep your cool.** "It's a play, not brain surgery" (M 23–25).
40. **Sleep, eat and know your limits.** Use your vacation (M 41–45).
41. **Failure is part of learning.** Come back to the problem (B 24, 31).

## Shop wisdom [General knowledge]

42. **Measure twice, cut once.**
43. **Buy two if you can only afford to lose one.** Always know where the spare is.
44. **Label everything**: storage bins, wet paint, rented items, "do not use" props, and the contents of every mixed container.
45. **Photograph everything before you change it**: rentals at pickup, stock before alteration, the set after dressing.
46. **Leave the shop cleaner than you found it.** The next person's safety depends on it.
47. **Keep receipts the moment you get them.**
48. **Test the finish on a scrap first**: paint, glue, sealer and solvent all react differently on different materials.
49. **If you're wondering whether it's safe, it isn't yet.** Stop and ask.
50. **Build relationships.** Most hard props are found through someone you know.

## For the classroom

- **Pick three.** Each student chooses the three rules they think matter most and writes about a time one of them applied (or would have helped).
- **Rule stories.** Assign each student one rule to trace back to its source chapter and present the story or example behind it.
- **Write rule 51.** After a production, each student proposes one new rule from what they learned and defends it to the class.
