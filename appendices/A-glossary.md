# Appendix A — Glossary

Definitions are written for this guide, drawing on Mussman's glossary (M 163–180), terminology throughout Hart (H), James's period-object dictionary (J), and Harrison & Honesty's fake-food materials (FF). Chapter numbers point to where a term is discussed.

---

**A-B foam** — Two-part polyurethane foam that expands as it cures; rigid or flexible. Isocyanate hazard. (ch. 21)
**Absorption (slip) casting** — Casting in a dry plaster mold that absorbs water so a skin forms on the walls; excess is poured out to leave a hollow cast. (ch. 21)
**Acrylic (PMMA)** — Clear, rigid thermoplastic sheet (Plexiglas, etc.); also the binder in acrylic paint. (chs. 16, 24)
**Action prop** — Broadway/film term for a hand prop. (ch. 1)
**Actuals** — The performance versions of props, as opposed to rehearsal props. (chs. 1, 7)
**Alginate** — Seaweed-derived, skin-safe molding material used for life casting; shrinks within hours. (ch. 21)
**Alkyd** — Modern oil-based paint; adheres to many surfaces; often used as primer. (ch. 24)
**Anachronism** — An object, word or behavior that couldn't belong to the production's period. Check both when the object was introduced and when it was common. (ch. 27)
**Apron** — (1) Stage area in front of the main curtain. (2) Rails connecting table legs under the top. (chs. 4, 14)
**Architect's scale** — Triangular ruler for reading scale drawings (e.g., ½" = 1'-0"). (ch. 12)
**Arena stage** — Audience on all four sides; props must read from every angle. (ch. 2)
**Argand lamp** — Oil lamp with a tubular wick and central air draft (1784), soon fitted with a glass chimney. Far brighter and cleaner than earlier lamps. (ch. 27; App. E)
**Armature** — Internal skeleton supporting a sculpture or shell. (chs. 17, 20)
**Armorer** — Licensed specialist responsible for firearms on film/stage productions. (ch. 26)
**Auto-body filler (Bondo)** — Polyester-resin paste that cures hard; sandable filler and sculpting skin. Toxic fumes/dust. (chs. 21, 23)
**Background prop** — Film/TV prop seen only at a distance; often less detailed. (ch. 1)
**Baker's clay** — See *salt dough*. (chs. 20, 28)
**Base coat** — The first flat "average" color layer of a paint treatment. (ch. 24)
**Batting** — Fluffy sheet padding (polyester or cotton) used in upholstery over foam. (ch. 18)
**Beauty (impression) coat** — First thin layer of a mold or cast material, applied carefully to capture detail without bubbles. (ch. 21)
**Betty lamp** — Covered iron or tin oil lamp with a hanging hook and a wick pick on a chain; common in colonial America. (ch. 27)
**Bias** — 45° to the grain of woven fabric; the stretchy direction. (ch. 18)
**Bleed-through** — Light or image showing through something meant to be opaque. (M)
**Blocking** — The movements given to actors by the director; props shape it and are shaped by it. (ch. 1)
**Board foot** — Lumber volume unit: L × W × T (inches) ÷ 144. (ch. 14)
**Bole** — Colored base layer under metal leaf (traditionally clay; usually paint in props). (ch. 24)
**Bounce** — Light reflected off light-colored objects, making them read brighter. (M; ch. 24)
**Box mold** — Walls built around a master, filled with mold material. (ch. 21)
**Break the edges** — Lightly round or chamfer edges so they won't splinter or cut. (ch. 14)
**Breakaway** — A prop designed to break on cue — either reassembling (rigged) or single-use (e.g., sugar glass). (chs. 1, 26)
**Brush-up mold** — Mold made by brushing on successive coats of rubber; usually supported by a mother mold. (ch. 21)
**Buck** — Positive form over which plastic is vacuum-formed. (ch. 16)
**Buckram** — Starch-stiffened millinery fabric, softened with water and dried over a form. (ch. 22)
**Burning an inch** — Measuring from the 1" mark of a tape to avoid hook error. (ch. 12)
**Cambric** — Black fabric dust cover stapled under upholstered furniture. (ch. 18)
**Canton** — The upper quarter of a flag nearest the staff. (ch. 27)
**Carcass** — Box- or case-type furniture (dressers, wardrobes) as opposed to frame furniture. (ch. 14)
**Cast / positive** — The copy produced from a mold. (ch. 21)
**Caul** — Board placed between a clamp and the work to protect it and spread pressure. (ch. 9)
**Celastic / Formfast** — Solvent-activated drapeable sheet that hardens; excellent drape, heavy solvent hazard. (ch. 22)
**Chatelaine** — A waist clasp or chains holding keys, a watch, scissors and other small necessities. (ch. 27)
**Chicken wire** — Galvanized hex mesh used as a formable skeleton under papier-mâché and other skins. (ch. 17)
**Cleat** — A small strip that strengthens a joint or supports a shelf. (chs. 14, 17)
**Clove hitch** — Knot for tying rope to a pipe or pole. (ch. 17)
**Commando cloth / duvetyn** — Matte black napped fabric used for masking and furniture skirts. (ch. 18)
**Complementary colors** — Opposites on the color wheel; mixing them neutralizes saturation. (ch. 24)
**Composite / laminate** — Fibrous reinforcement in a hardened polymer matrix (papier-mâché, fiberglass). (ch. 22)
**Compression leg** — A leg placed under the load so the material, not the fasteners, carries it. (ch. 17)
**Consumable / perishable** — A prop used up each performance (food, blood, torn paper). (ch. 1)
**Contact cement** — Adhesive applied to both surfaces, allowed to dry, then pressed together. (ch. 13)
**Contingency** — Budget or time reserve (typically 20–25%) for the unforeseen. (ch. 5)
**Co-production** — A show built by one theatre and transferred to a partner theatre. (ch. 7)
**Costume prop ("propstume")** — An accessory that completes a costume (watch, holster, mask). (ch. 1)
**Counter-rake** — Building a prop's base on an opposite slope so it sits level on a raked stage. (ch. 11)
**Crash box** — Box of junk shaken or dropped offstage for a live crash sound. (ch. 26)
**Creme wax** — Opaque candle wax. Mixed with gel wax, it makes firmer, faster-setting fake cheese, custard and fillings; on its own it grates like cheddar. (ch. 28)
**Critical measurement** — A dimension that must be exact because it relates to another element (doorway, contents). (chs. 4, 12)
**CrystalGel** — Rosco's translucent, flexible, water-based coating and adhesive gel. FF's all-purpose medium for paper lettuce, icing, glazes and wet sheen. (chs. 23, 28)
**Cue to cue** — Tech rehearsal skipping from cue to cue. (M)
**Cuir bouilli** — Leather hardened by brief boiling or low baking. (ch. 22)
**Cure** — Chemical hardening of an adhesive, resin, or paint (vs. drying by evaporation). (chs. 13, 21)
**Cut list** — List of every part and its dimensions for a build. (ch. 12)
**Dado** — Channel cut across the grain. (ch. 14)
**Dart** — A wedge removed from fabric and sewn closed to create shape. (ch. 19)
**Decal** — Printed image on water-slide (or heat) transfer paper applied to a surface. (ch. 25)
**Deck** — The stage floor. (M)
**Development / stretchout** — Flat pattern produced by unfolding a 3D shape's surfaces. (ch. 19)
**Diapering / greebles / nurnies** — Applied repetitive or random surface detail that makes a surface read as complex. (ch. 13)
**Distress / age / weather / antique** — Making a prop look used, damaged, weather-beaten, or old-but-cared-for. (chs. 23, 24)
**Drybrushing** — Dragging a nearly dry brush so paint catches raised texture. (ch. 24)
**Dummy (static) prop** — Looks real but doesn't function. (ch. 1)
**Dutchman** — Strip glued over the seam between flats; also old term for wheat paste. (M; ch. 24)
**Dye** — Colorant that soaks into a material (vs. paint, which coats). (ch. 24)
**Edible inset** — A piece of real food set into an otherwise fake dish, behind a food-safe barrier, for an actor to eat. (chs. 26, 28)
**Elastomer** — A rubbery polymer. (ch. 16)
**Elevation** — Scale front or side drawing of scenery or a prop. (ch. 4)
**Encapso K** — Smooth-On's water-clear encapsulation rubber (1:1, 2-hr pot life, 24-hr cure). Tinted by the drop for fake drinks. (ch. 28)
**EVA foam** — Closed-cell "craft foam"; heat-formable; used for lightweight armor and masks. (chs. 16, 19)
**Expansion ratio** — How many times its liquid volume an expanding foam grows. FlexFoam-iT! III is about 15×, V about 11×. (chs. 21, 28)
**Faux bois / graining** — Painting a surface to imitate wood grain. (ch. 24)
**Faux finish** — Painting one material to look like another. (ch. 24)
**Fence** — (1) Guide on a machine that the work rides against. (2) Divider wall used when making multi-part brush-up molds. (chs. 9, 21)
**FEV (French enamel varnish)** — Shellac tinted with alcohol-soluble dye or metallic powder, thinned with alcohol. FF's food glaze is 1 shellac : 10 alcohol plus leather dye. (chs. 24, 28)
**Fiberglass (GRP)** — Glass-fiber cloth or mat laminated in resin. (ch. 22)
**Fillet weld** — Weld laid on top of a joint, typically at tees and corners. (ch. 15)
**Fishplate / scab** — Metal / wood plate across an end-to-end joint. (ch. 17)
**Flame retardant / IFR** — Treated to slow burning / inherently flame-retardant material. (chs. 10, 23)
**Flashing** — Thin fin of cast material at a mold's parting line. (ch. 21)
**Flash-off** — Letting a sprayed or brushed release agent or solvent evaporate before pouring. (ch. 28)
**Flat (soft/hard)** — Framed scenic wall unit covered with fabric (soft) or a sheet material (hard). (chs. 14, 17)
**Flocking** — Applying short fibers to an adhesive surface for a velvety or grassy texture. (ch. 23)
**Fly / hoist (flag)** — The edge of a flag farthest from (fly) and nearest to (hoist) the staff. Proportions are given as hoist : fly. (ch. 27)
**Footprint** — The floor area an object covers. (M; ch. 4)
**Former** — Profile-cut rib that defines a curved surface; joined by stringers. (chs. 14, 17)
**Fosshape** — Heat-activated nonwoven that stiffens when steamed; shrinks. (ch. 22)
**Fourth wall** — The imaginary wall between audience and a realistic set. (M)
**Gaff (gaffer's) tape** — Strong, low-residue cloth tape. (ch. 13)
**Gauge** — Thickness designation for sheet metal (weight-based; lower = thicker). (ch. 15)
**Gel coat** — Polyester surface resin used as the first or last coat in fiberglass work. (ch. 22)
**Gel wax** — Translucent candle wax, poured and tinted to fake liquids, sauces and fillings. Pour at 160–170°F; never above 220°F. (ch. 28)
**Gimp** — Decorative trim covering staples on upholstery. (ch. 18)
**Glaze vs. wash** — Translucent paint thinned with clear medium (glaze) or with vehicle/water (wash). (ch. 24)
**Glossy Wood Tone** — Design Master aerosol tint. FF's standard way to fake oven browning on crusts, edges and meat. (ch. 28)
**Glow tape** — Phosphorescent tape marking edges for blackout visibility. (M)
**Grain** — Directional fibers (wood, fabric, paper, some metals); materials are stronger along it. (chs. 14, 17, 18)
**Green glue** — Water-based contact cement; safe on polystyrene foam. (chs. 13, 20)
**Green stage** — Point at which a cast or laminate holds shape but is still slightly flexible — ideal for demolding. (ch. 21)
**Ground plan** — Scale top-view drawing of the set on the stage. (ch. 4)
**Gusset** — (1) Plate reinforcing a structural joint. (2) Fabric insert that adds fullness. (chs. 17, 19)
**Hand prop** — A prop handled by an actor. (ch. 1)
**Heads!** — Warning shout when something falls from above. (M)
**Hero prop** — Film/TV prop built for close-ups. (ch. 1)
**Hide the uglies** — Final pass removing visual distractions before audiences arrive. (ch. 7)
**Hot glue (hot melt)** — Thermoplastic adhesive applied with a heated gun; low- and high-melt varieties. (ch. 13)
**Hot knife** — Electrically heated blade that melts through wax or foam for smooth cut faces. (ch. 28)
**Hot work** — Welding, cutting, grinding — anything producing sparks or flame; often permit-controlled. (chs. 10, 15)
**Hue / saturation / value** — Color name / vividness / lightness. (ch. 24)
**Isocyanate** — Hazardous component of polyurethane foams and resins; causes asthma and sensitization; no odor warning. (chs. 10, 21)
**Jig** — Device that holds a fixed relationship between tool and work or between parts, for accuracy and repetition. (ch. 12)
**Kerf** — Width of the cut a blade makes. (chs. 9, 12)
**Kerfing** — Cutting parallel slots partway through stock so it bends. (ch. 14)
**Kickback** — Material violently thrown back by a saw blade. (ch. 14)
**Kitbashing** — Building with parts scavenged from other objects or kits. (chs. 6, 13)
**Lashing (diagonal/shear)** — Rope bindings joining poles. (ch. 17)
**Lauan** — Thin, light tropical plywood; buy FSC-certified. (ch. 14)
**Load-in / load-out** — Moving production elements into / out of the venue. (ch. 7)
**Lorem ipsum** — Placeholder text for paper props that won't be read. (ch. 25)
**Lorgnette** — Spectacles on a handle, held up to the eyes; often more fashion than need. (ch. 27)
**Maquette** — Small-scale sculptural model used as reference. (ch. 20)
**Master** — The original object being molded. (ch. 21)
**Matchlock / wheel lock / flintlock** — Successive firearm ignition mechanisms (1400s, early 1500s, early 1600s), useful for dating period weapons. (chs. 26, 27)
**MDF / MDO** — Medium-density fiberboard (smooth, grainless) / overlaid plywood with MDF faces. (ch. 14)
**Mock-up** — Full-scale stand-in built from cheap material to test size, use, and proportion. (chs. 7, 12)
**Monster mud** — Latex paint + joint compound, often with draped burlap, for crusty textures. (ch. 23)
**Mortise and tenon** — Classic joint: a tongue (tenon) glued into a socket (mortise). (ch. 14)
**Mortite** — Pliable caulking putty used to anchor dressing temporarily. (chs. 7, 13)
**Mother mold (jacket)** — Rigid shell supporting a flexible mold. (ch. 21)
**Nap / pile** — Raised fibers on fabric (velvet); directional. (ch. 18)
**Needs and means** — Hart's method: define everything the prop must do, then meet those needs with available resources. (ch. 11)
**NIOSH rating** — US certification for respirators (e.g., N95, P100). (ch. 10)
**Nominal vs. actual** — Lumber's name size vs. real size (a 2×4 is 1½" × 3½"). (ch. 14)
**Oil clay (plastilina)** — Non-drying modeling clay used for masters; use sulfur-free under silicone. (ch. 20)
**Orthographic projection** — Drafting views (front, side, top) without perspective. (chs. 4, 12)
**Papier-mâché** — Paper strips or pulp laminated with paste or glue into a hard shell. (ch. 22)
**Parting line** — Where mold pieces meet. (ch. 21)
**Pattern book** — A published collection of furniture designs (Chippendale, Hepplewhite, Sheraton) that spread a style; many style names come from them. (ch. 27)
**PCL (polycaprolactone)** — Plastic that softens in hot water for hand-modeling (InstaMorph, etc.). (chs. 16, 20)
**Pepakura** — Unfolding 3D models into printable paper patterns. (ch. 19)
**Permeation chart** — Glove manufacturer's data on how long a glove resists specific chemicals. (ch. 10)
**Personal prop** — A prop an actor keeps on their person; not handled by crew. (ch. 1)
**Petty cash** — Cash advance for small purchases, reconciled with receipts. (ch. 5)
**Piece mold** — Multi-part reusable rigid mold, needed for undercuts. (ch. 21)
**Pilot hole** — Pre-drilled hole that prevents splitting when driving a screw. (chs. 13, 14)
**Pince-nez** — Eyeglasses without sidepieces that grip the bridge of the nose (mid-1800s). (ch. 27)
**Piping / welt** — Fabric-covered cord sewn into upholstery seams. (ch. 18)
**Plaster bandage** — Plaster-impregnated gauze; used for mother molds. (ch. 21)
**Pot life** — How long a mixed two-part material stays workable before it starts to set. (chs. 21, 28)
**Practical** — A prop that works as in real life (lamp lights, tap runs). (chs. 1, 26)
**Preset** — Where props are placed before the show or scene begins. (M; ch. 7)
**Preview** — Performance for a paying audience before official opening, when changes may still occur. (M)
**Primary source** — Evidence from the period itself: photographs, catalogs, advertisements, patents, paintings, surviving objects. (ch. 27)
**Prime coat** — Sealing first coat that gives later paint a uniform surface. (ch. 24)
**Production meeting** — Regular meeting of department heads and creative team. (ch. 3)
**Project triangle** — Fast, cheap, good: pick two. (ch. 5)
**Prop bible** — Binder (or shared folder) holding all information for a show's props. (ch. 4)
**Prop list** — Master record of every prop in the production; "the most important document." (ch. 4)
**Prop request deadline** — Cutoff for major prop additions before tech. (ch. 7)
**Prop table** — Labeled, lit offstage table where hand props live during a show. (ch. 7)
**Proscenium** — Stage framed by an arch with the audience on one side. (ch. 2)
**Pull** — Take an item from stock. (chs. 6, M)
**Purchase order (PO)** — Authorization document for purchases charged to an account. (ch. 5)
**PVA** — (1) Polyvinyl *acetate*: white and yellow glues. (2) Polyvinyl *alcohol*: a film-forming mold release. Don't confuse them. (chs. 13, 21)
**Rabbet** — L-shaped notch along an edge or end. (ch. 14)
**Rake** — Sloped stage floor. (ch. 11)
**Red flag** — Mussman's term for raising a concern early enough to solve it. (ch. 3)
**Registration keys** — Bumps and sockets that align mold halves. (ch. 21)
**Rehearsal prop** — Stand-in matching size and function, used before performance props are ready. (chs. 1, 7)
**Rehearsal report** — Stage manager's daily report of rehearsal events and departmental notes. (chs. 3, 7)
**Release agent** — Coating that prevents a cast from sticking to its mold. (ch. 21)
**Relief cut** — A slit cut into a flexible mold to free a curved or undercut master. Tape it closed before casting. (chs. 21, 28)
**Rendering** — Perspective color drawing of the set; sizes may be distorted. (ch. 4)
**Resaw** — Cutting a board through its thickness. (ch. 14)
**Revival style** — A later reproduction of an earlier style (e.g., Victorian Louis XV). It can itself be period-accurate. (ch. 27)
**Rip / crosscut** — Cut along / across the grain. (ch. 14)
**Riving knife** — Blade-mounted fin that prevents a kerf from closing and causing kickback. (ch. 14)
**Running prop** — Item replenished at intervals (batteries, newspapers). (ch. 1)
**Rush light** — A rush soaked in fat and burned in an iron clip; cheap, dim and in constant need of tending. (ch. 27)
**Sad iron** — A heavy, solid flatiron heated on a stove; used into the 1930s in rural areas. (App. E)
**Saddle stitch** — Two-needle hand stitch for leather. (ch. 18)
**Salt dough** — Flour, salt and water (often with oil and alum) kneaded and hardened by baking or microwaving. Also called baker's clay. Used for crusts, dumplings and fried textures. (chs. 20, 28)
**Scenic dope** — Homemade texture paste (latex paint, latex caulk, joint compound). (ch. 23)
**Scenic paint** — Highly pigmented, usually flat paint made for scenery. (ch. 24)
**Scumbling** — Stippling a nearly dry brush to build soft highlights. (ch. 24)
**SDS (formerly MSDS)** — Safety Data Sheet listing a product's hazards and precautions. (ch. 10)
**Selvage** — Factory edge of fabric. (ch. 18)
**Sensitizer** — Chemical that can cause permanent allergy after repeated (or even single) exposure. (ch. 10)
**Set decoration** — Film/TV department responsible for set props and dressing. (chs. 1, 2)
**Set dressing** — Items on the set that create atmosphere but aren't handled. (ch. 1)
**Set piece** — Scenic element built by the scene shop (vs. set prop). (ch. 1)
**Set prop** — Objects on the set, mostly furniture. (ch. 1)
**Shore hardness** — Hardness scale for rubbers and plastics (OO, A, D). (ch. 21)
**Sightlines** — The audience's lines of view; can require shortening props on thrust/arena stages. (ch. 11)
**Silicone rubber** — Flexible, long-life mold rubber (tin- or platinum-cure). (ch. 21)
**Skim coat** — Thin overall layer of filler sanded smooth. (ch. 23)
**Slop sink** — Sink for paint and plaster cleanup, separate from food/clean uses. (ch. 9)
**Slush casting** — Swirling casting material to coat a mold's walls for a hollow cast. (ch. 21)
**Spike / spike tape** — Mark (and narrow colored tape) showing where furniture sits onstage. (M; ch. 13)
**Spill** — A strip of wood or paper lit at the fire to carry a flame, used before matches. (ch. 27)
**Sprue / gate / vent** — Pour channel / passage into a mold cavity / air escape. (ch. 21)
**Sticketta** — Rounded wooden tool for working wet leather over a form. (ch. 22)
**Stock** — A theatre's stored collection of props, furniture, and materials. (ch. 8)
**Strike** — Removing a production from the venue after closing. (ch. 8)
**Strongback** — L-shaped beam of two boards (one flat, one on edge) that resists bending both ways. (ch. 17)
**Stunt prop** — Soft or breakaway version of a prop for action scenes. (ch. 1)
**Swatch** — Small fabric (or paint) sample for approval. (ch. 18)
**Tack weld** — Small temporary weld holding parts in position. (ch. 15)
**Tech out** — Drawing the hidden structure of a design before building. (ch. 17)
**Tech (technical rehearsals)** — Rehearsals integrating all technical elements before dress. (ch. 7)
**Temples (spectacles)** — The sidepieces of eyeglasses that rest on the ears; introduced c. 1727. (ch. 27)
**Tester** — A canopy over a bed (or over an altar or pulpit). (App. E)
**Thai Unryu paper** — Thin, translucent mulberry paper with long fibres that tears to a soft edge. Fake vegetables, spinach and spring-roll wrappers. (ch. 28)
**Thermoplastic / thermoset** — Plastic that re-softens with heat / cures permanently. (ch. 16)
**Thrust stage** — Audience on three sides. (ch. 2)
**Tie line** — Thin black cotton cord for tying and securing. (chs. 7, M)
**TPI (teeth per inch)** — Saw blade tooth count; higher = smoother, slower. (ch. 9)
**Trestle table** — Boards laid on removable supports; the medieval hall table, taken apart after meals. (App. E)
**Trim prop** — Set props hung on walls (curtains, pictures). (ch. 1)
**Tufting** — Stitching through upholstery layers, often with buttons, in a pattern. (ch. 18)
**Turnaround time** — Calendar time until a prop is ready, including drying, shopping, shipping. (ch. 5)
**Undercut** — A recess that locks a cast in a rigid mold. (ch. 21)
**Upstaging (by a prop)** — A prop drawing focus inappropriately. (M)
**Vacuum forming** — Pulling heated plastic sheet over a buck with vacuum. (ch. 16)
**Vegetable-tanned leather** — Leather that can be carved, tooled, and wet-molded. (chs. 18, 22)
**Wagon (truck)** — Rolling platform used for scene shifts; props on wagons must be secured. (M; ch. 7)
**Wale** — Stiffening rib along a span. (ch. 17)
**Waste mold** — Rigid mold destroyed to release a single cast. (ch. 21)
**Waste side** — The side of a cut line where the kerf should fall. (ch. 12)
**Wet blending** — Blending wet paint colors on the surface without hard edges. (ch. 24)
**Wet edge** — Keeping the leading edge of paint wet to avoid lap marks. (ch. 24)
**White model** — Uncolored scale model for checking massing and fit. (ch. 4)
**Wiggle wood (bending ply)** — Plywood that bends in one direction ("column" or "barrel"). (ch. 14)
**XPS (extruded polystyrene)** — Pink or blue rigid insulation foam. Laminated and carved for bread, roasts, cakes and other large props. (chs. 16, 20, 28)
